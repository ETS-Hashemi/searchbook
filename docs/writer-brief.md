## Working method

* Write the chapter in a few large writes, not many tiny edits.
* Write the Python first, run it, and take every number in the worked example from it.
* Then the figures, then the text. Compile with `cd Overleaf && ./build.sh chNN-slug` and
  read `build/only-chNN-slug.log` for errors; `pdftotext build/only-chNN-slug.pdf - | less`
  to proofread; `pdftoppm -r 50 -png -f P -l P build/only-chNN-slug.pdf /tmp/pg` and view a
  page image if you want to check a figure.
* Macro/function names in `\SetKwFunction`, `\newcommand` and listing labels must be unique
  across the book: prefix them with your chapter topic (e.g. `\SetKwFunction{CbsHighLevel}{...}`).
  Prefer no new `\newcommand` at all.
* ASCII only inside listings and `.dat` files. Use `--` for en-dashes in text, `\%` for percent.
* Cross-references to other chapters use the labels in the table below (`\cref{ch:ch09}`),
  to the study plan `\cref{ch:appA}`, to the maths refresher `\cref{ch:appB}`. Never claim
  what another chapter contains beyond what the table says.
* Finish only when: build status 0, no errors, no undefined labels of your own, the self-test
  passes, and the chapter has every mandatory section of the template.

## The book at a glance (labels and scope of every chapter)

Part I — Foundations
* `ch:ch01` **Planning for Drone Swarms.** The problem; planned inter-drone conflicts vs
  unexpected moving obstacles; global/local/prediction/replanning layers (preview of the
  hybrid architecture); map of all algorithms with their priority; established results vs open
  research; how to read the book.
* `ch:ch02` **The Toolbox.** Graphs, grids (4/8-connected), configuration space and obstacle
  inflation, space-time (time-expanded) states with wait actions, paths/plans/trajectories and
  their costs (length, makespan, sum of costs), point-mass and double-integrator drone models,
  velocity/acceleration limits, discretisation, basic 2D/3D geometry (dot product, distance to
  segment, ray–circle intersection, Minkowski sum), Gaussians and covariance, big-O, priority
  queues, the Python stack used in the book.

Part II — Single-Agent Graph Search
* `ch:ch03` **Dijkstra.** Non-negative weights, priority queue, settled-node invariant and
  proof, complexity, uniform-cost search, early exit, backward Dijkstra as an exact heuristic.
* `ch:ch04` **A\*.** f=g+h, admissible and consistent heuristics (with proofs), optimality
  theorem, grid heuristics (Manhattan, octile, Euclidean), tie-breaking, weighted A\* and its
  bound, **space-time A\*** (state = vertex and time, wait action, vertex/edge constraints,
  goal-stay handling), open/closed-set visualisation.
* `ch:ch05` **LPA\* and D\* Lite.** g/rhs values, local consistency, keys, UpdateVertex,
  ComputeShortestPath, D\* Lite backward search with key modifier k_m, replanning after
  obstacle changes, comparison with re-running A\*.
* `ch:ch06` **ARA\*.** Anytime search, epsilon schedule, INCONS list, bounds, Anytime D\* pointer.

Part III — Multi-Agent Path Finding
* `ch:ch07` **The MAPF problem.** Formal definition (Stern et al. 2019), actions, plans,
  vertex/edge(swap)/following/cycle conflicts, sum-of-costs and makespan, stay-at-target,
  NP-hardness, solver families, conflict detection, benchmarks and instance files.
* `ch:ch08` **Prioritized planning and space-time A\*.** Cooperative A\*, reservation tables,
  hierarchical and windowed variants, priority orderings, incompleteness and suboptimality
  with counterexamples, well-formed instances, priority-based search pointer.
* `ch:ch09` **CBS.** Constraint tree, high-level best-first search on cost, low-level
  constrained space-time A\*, conflict splitting, worked example with the tree drawn,
  optimality/completeness, ICBS improvements (cardinal conflicts, bypass), symmetry and
  heuristics pointers, implementation details.
* `ch:ch10` **ECBS.** Bounded suboptimality, focal search (A\*-epsilon), low-level and
  high-level focal lists, lower bounds, w-suboptimality proof, benchmark vs CBS, EECBS pointer.
* `ch:ch11` **M\*, Push-and-Swap, Push-and-Rotate.** Joint state space blow-up, subdimensional
  expansion and collision sets, M\* properties, push/swap/rotate primitives, completeness
  conditions, comparison of all MAPF methods in the book.

Part IV — Local and Reactive Collision Avoidance
* `ch:ch12` **Velocity obstacles.** Relative position/velocity, collision cone, VO geometry
  (tangents to the Minkowski disc), time-to-collision, truncated VO with horizon tau, choosing
  a velocity, oscillation problem, 3D cone.
* `ch:ch13` **RVO and ORCA.** Reciprocity, RVO definition, ORCA half-plane derivation
  (smallest change u, normal n, half responsibility), linear program over half-planes,
  infeasibility handling, non-cooperative obstacles (full responsibility), 3D ORCA, simulation
  comparison no-avoidance/VO/ORCA.
* `ch:ch14` **DWA.** Velocity space, dynamic window from acceleration limits, admissible
  velocities (stopping distance), rollouts, heading/clearance/velocity objective, tuning,
  failure cases.
* `ch:ch15` **APF.** Attractive and repulsive potentials (Khatib), gradient as force, local
  minima, oscillations, goals near obstacles, remedies, inter-agent repulsion.

Part V — Sampling-Based Motion Planning
* `ch:ch16` **RRT.** Sample/Nearest/Steer/CollisionFree, goal bias, probabilistic completeness,
  RRT-Connect, collision checking, KD-trees, smoothing, 3D use.
* `ch:ch17` **RRT\* and Informed RRT\*.** Near/ChooseParent/Rewire, connection radius,
  asymptotic optimality, informed ellipsoidal sampling, convergence plots, BIT\* pointer.

Part VI — Tracking and Prediction
* `ch:ch18` **Kalman filter.** Linear-Gaussian model, predict/update, gain, constant-velocity
  model and white-noise-acceleration Q, tuning, gating, missing measurements, worked numbers.
* `ch:ch19` **EKF, UKF, particle filter.** Jacobians, sigma points and weights, sequential
  importance resampling, ESS, comparison on a turning target, when to use which.
* `ch:ch20` **Trajectory prediction.** Problem, ADE/FDE, constant-velocity/acceleration and
  KF baselines, LSTM cells and sequence-to-sequence prediction, Social-LSTM idea,
  Transformers and attention over agents, multimodality and uncertainty, evaluation protocol,
  feeding predictions with uncertainty into planners.

Part VII — Optimization and Control
* `ch:ch21` **MPC.** Receding horizon, double-integrator model, quadratic cost, box and
  collision constraints, QP formulation, soft constraints and slacks, horizon/weight tuning,
  formation/communication-distance constraints.
* `ch:ch22` **MILP.** LP recap, binaries and big-M obstacle avoidance, inter-vehicle
  separation, branch-and-bound intuition, scipy `milp`, MAPF as ILP pointer, cost of exactness.
* `ch:ch23` **Consensus and formation control.** Graph Laplacian, consensus dynamics and
  convergence, leader–follower, displacement-based formation, formation error, flocking rules,
  communication-radius/connectivity constraints.

Part VIII — Putting It Together
* `ch:ch24` **The hybrid collision-avoidance architecture.** Four layers (CBS/ECBS,
  KF/LSTM prediction, ORCA/DWA, D\* Lite/A\* replanning), decision logic and state machine,
  safety horizon and time-to-collision trigger, reconnection to the nominal path, replanning
  triggers, conflict re-check, formation/communication constraints, prediction-aware
  (uncertainty-inflated) constraints, what guarantees survive, research directions.
* `ch:ch25` **Evaluating a hybrid planner.** Experiment matrix (drones, intruders, prediction
  quality, avoidance strategy, constraints), metric definitions (collision rate, minimum
  separation, path length, travel time, makespan, sum of costs, replanning count,
  computation time, formation error, communication violations), scenario generation,
  statistics and reporting, reproducibility.

Appendices: `ch:appA` twelve-week study plan and traceability table; `ch:appB` mathematical
refresher; `ch:appC` hints and solutions.
