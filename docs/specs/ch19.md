# Chapter 19 — Nonlinear Filtering: EKF, UKF and Particle Filters (EKF High, UKF Medium, PF Medium; Week 9)
File Overleaf/chapters/ch19-nonlinear-filters.tex, label ch:ch19. Algorithm template (three algorithms; give each its own algorithm environment and worked mini-example). Target 15-17 pages.
Anchors: Thrun et al. 2005 (ch. 3-4); Julier & Uhlmann 1997 and 2004; Wan & van der Merwe 2000; Gordon, Salmond & Smith 1993; Arulampalam et al. 2002; Bar-Shalom et al. 2001 (coordinated-turn model).
Must cover: why nonlinearity arises (a turning drone: the coordinated-turn model with turn rate omega in the state; range-bearing measurements from a sensor; both given with formulas); the EXTENDED KALMAN FILTER: linearise f and h with Jacobians F_k = df/dx at the estimate and H_k = dh/dx at the prediction, then run the KF equations; pseudocode; the Jacobians of the coordinated-turn model and of range-bearing, written out; a worked mini-example (numbers from code); where the EKF fails (strong nonlinearity, poor initial estimate, figure showing the linearisation error); the UNSCENTED KALMAN FILTER: the unscented transform (2n+1 sigma points x_0 = mean, x_i = mean +/- (sqrt((n+lambda) P))_i, weights W_m and W_c with alpha, beta, kappa and lambda = alpha^2 (n+kappa) - n; state the standard choices alpha = 1e-3..1, beta = 2, kappa = 0 or 3-n), propagate sigma points through f and h, recombine mean and covariance, the cross-covariance and the gain; pseudocode; matrix square root via Cholesky; the comparison EKF vs UKF on the turning target (generated figure and RMSE table); the PARTICLE FILTER (sequential importance resampling / bootstrap filter): particles and weights, propagate through the motion model with sampled noise, weight by the measurement likelihood, normalise, RESAMPLE (systematic resampling with pseudocode), the effective sample size N_eff = 1/sum(w^2) and resampling when it drops below a threshold, degeneracy and sample impoverishment; a worked example where the posterior is multimodal (an intruder that may have turned left or right behind an occlusion) with a figure of the particle cloud splitting; pseudocode; properties and costs of all three (table: assumptions, cost per step, handles multimodality?, tuning burden, typical use), and a decision guide for "which filter for which intruder model"; implementation notes (numerical issues with Cholesky, angle wrapping in bearing innovations, particle count, vectorising with NumPy, log-weights); pitfalls (Jacobian evaluated at the wrong point, forgetting to wrap angles, resampling every step, too few particles in 6D); drone box (the prediction layer for manoeuvring intruders; particles as the natural input to prediction-aware constraints with uncertainty in ch:ch24); 8 exercises incl. a coding exercise (track a turning drone with EKF, UKF and PF, compare RMSE) and a derivation (the coordinated-turn Jacobian).
