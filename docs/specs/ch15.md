# Chapter 15 — Artificial Potential Fields (priority High; Week 7)
File Overleaf/chapters/ch15-potential-fields.tex, label ch:ch15. Algorithm template. Target 11-13 pages.
Anchors: Khatib, "Real-Time Obstacle Avoidance for Manipulators and Mobile Robots", IJRR 5(1) 1986, 90-98; Koren & Borenstein, ICRA 1991 (limitations); Reynolds 1987 (flocking, for the swarm connection).
Must cover: the idea - the goal attracts, obstacles repel, the robot follows the negative gradient; the standard potentials with formulas: attractive U_att(q) = 1/2 * k_att * ||q - q_goal||^2 (quadratic) or conic far away (and why the hybrid is used), repulsive U_rep(q) = 1/2 * k_rep * (1/rho(q) - 1/rho_0)^2 for rho(q) <= rho_0 and 0 otherwise, where rho is the distance to the obstacle; the forces as negative gradients, with the derivations; a figure of the potential surface (pgfplots 3D or a contour plot from generated data) and a figure of the force field with a trajectory; pseudocode of the gradient-descent controller with a velocity limit; a worked example from the code (a drone, one obstacle, the numbers of the two forces at three points, in a table); the FAILURE MODES, each with its own figure and a runnable example: (1) LOCAL MINIMA where attractive and repulsive forces cancel (the classic obstacle-between-robot-and-goal case), (2) goals non-reachable with obstacles nearby (GNRON) where the repulsive force at the goal prevents arrival, and the standard fix (multiply the repulsive term by ||q - q_goal||^n), (3) oscillation in narrow passages and with discrete time steps, (4) no passage between closely spaced obstacles; the Koren-Borenstein critique summarised honestly; remedies: navigation functions (Rimon-Koditschek, mentioned with the sphere-world result), random walks/simulated annealing, harmonic potentials, and - most relevant here - using APF only as a LOCAL layer under a global plan; inter-agent repulsion for swarms and the connection to flocking (separation/alignment/cohesion, Reynolds) and to consensus (ch:ch23); a generated experiment: trajectories from many start positions in a field with two obstacles, showing the basin that falls into a local minimum (pgfplots); implementation notes (step size, force clipping, distance queries, the discrete-time instability); pitfalls; drone box (APF as the cheapest reactive layer and why the book prefers ORCA/DWA for the safety layer, with APF kept for formation-keeping and soft inter-agent spacing); 8 exercises incl. the Week-7 coding exercise (implement APF, create failure cases) and a derivation exercise.
