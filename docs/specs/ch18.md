Week 9: "Kalman/EKF tracking, state estimation, uncertainty. Simulate an external drone observed with noisy position/velocity measurements. Track it with a Kalman filter. Milestone: your planner receives a stable estimated state instead of raw noisy observations."
Must cover: the problem (estimate the state of an unknown drone from noisy measurements; why raw measurements are unusable for planning - figure of jittery raw positions vs the filtered track); the linear-Gaussian state-space model x_k = F x_{k-1} + B u_k + w_k, z_k = H x_k + v_k with w ~ N(0,Q), v ~ N(0,R) (use F for the transition matrix and H for the measurement matrix, and say so in a notation note since control texts use A and C); the Gaussian facts needed (affine transforms, conditioning) cited from ch:appB; the PREDICT step (mean and covariance) and the UPDATE step (innovation y = z - H x^-, innovation covariance S = H P^- H^T + R, gain K = P^- H^T S^{-1}, x = x^- + K y, P = (I - K H) P^-, and the Joseph form for numerical stability) - derive the update from Gaussian conditioning, and explain the gain as a weighting between prediction and measurement (1D example with numbers and a figure of the two Gaussians and their product); pseudocode; the CONSTANT-VELOCITY model for a drone in 2D and 3D (state [p, v], F with dt, H selecting position or position+velocity) and the white-noise-acceleration process noise Q = q * [[dt^3/3, dt^2/2],[dt^2/2, dt]] (per axis) with its derivation, plus the constant-acceleration model in one paragraph; a FULLY worked numeric example from the code: a 2D drone, 5 time steps, table of predicted mean, measurement, gain and updated mean/covariance (numbers from the code); tuning: what Q and R do (too small Q: sluggish; too large: noisy), the innovation as a diagnostic (whiteness, NIS), and a figure showing three q values on the same data; gating with the Mahalanobis distance (chi-square threshold) for rejecting outliers and for data association when several intruders exist; missing measurements (predict-only steps) and track initialisation from two measurements; uncertainty propagation for PREDICTION: propagating the covariance k steps ahead gives the growing ellipses that the safety layer (ch:ch24) and ORCA radius inflation use - figure of the ellipses; properties: the KF is the optimal (minimum mean-square-error) estimator for linear-Gaussian systems and the best linear estimator otherwise; observability in one paragraph; a generated experiment figure: true trajectory, noisy measurements, KF estimate, and the 2-sigma ellipses, plus RMSE vs q (pgfplots from .dat); implementation notes (numerical symmetry of P, Joseph form, units, time-varying dt); pitfalls (wrong Q scaling with dt, measuring velocity directly when only positions are available, resetting the filter on every outlier, confusing prediction covariance with estimation covariance); drone box (the prediction layer: tracks the intruder, delivers state + covariance to the local layer and to the constant-velocity predictor of ch:ch20); 8-10 exercises incl. the Week-9 coding exercise (simulate an external drone with noisy position/velocity measurements and track it) and derivations (the gain, Q for the CV model).
