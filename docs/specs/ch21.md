# Chapter 21 — Model Predictive Control (priority Essential; Week 11)

Anchors: Rawlings, Mayne & Diehl 2017; Mayne et al., Automatica 2000; Garcia, Prett & Morari 1989; Borrelli, Bemporad & Morari 2017; Boyd & Vandenberghe 2004 (QP); Zhu & Alonso-Mora 2019 (chance-constrained MAV avoidance); Mellinger & Kumar 2011 (quadrotor trajectories, pointer).
Week 11: "Receding-horizon optimization, hard/soft constraints, formation error, communication radius. Create an MPC or simplified constrained controller that follows a path while preserving a formation or distance limit. Milestone: constraints are explicitly enforced rather than handled only after violation."
Must cover: the receding-horizon principle (optimise a trajectory over N steps, apply the first input, shift, repeat; figure); the discrete-time double-integrator drone model from ch:ch02 (x = [p, v], u = a) and its matrices; the finite-horizon optimal control problem: cost sum_k (x_k - x_k^ref)^T Q (x_k - x_k^ref) + u_k^T R u_k + terminal cost (state clearly that Q, R here are cost weights, unlike the noise covariances of ch:ch18), subject to dynamics, input bounds |a| <= a_max, velocity bounds, and state constraints; CONSTRAINT TYPES that matter for the swarm: (1) obstacle/intruder avoidance ||p_k - o_k|| >= r_safe (non-convex) and its convexification by a linearised half-plane per step (figure), or by a sequence of convex corridors; (2) formation keeping: ||p_k^i - p_k^j - d_ij|| <= e_max; (3) communication range ||p_k^i - p_k^j|| <= R_comm; (4) soft constraints with slack variables and penalty weights, and when to use hard vs soft; the QP FORMULATION: stacking the horizon into a single vector, the condensed form (prediction matrices), the resulting quadratic program in standard form (write the matrices), and how to solve it (an active-set or interior-point solver; the book uses a small dense solver - either scipy.optimize.minimize with SLSQP on the condensed problem, or a projected-gradient/ADMM solver written in NumPy - state which and why); pseudocode of the MPC loop; a FULLY worked example with numbers from the code: a drone following a reference path while an intruder crosses, N = 10-20, showing the active constraint and the resulting deviation (figure with the reference, the MPC trajectory, the intruder and the safety disc; table of a few solved inputs); STABILITY and FEASIBILITY in one section, honestly at the level of a first course: terminal cost/terminal set ideas (Mayne et al.), recursive feasibility, why soft constraints avoid infeasibility, and what can go wrong; tuning: horizon N, weights, dt, with a generated figure showing the effect of N and of the avoidance weight; prediction-aware constraints: replacing the intruder's point position with the predicted mean plus an uncertainty-inflated radius from ch:ch18/ch:ch20 (chance constraint idea, Zhu & Alonso-Mora); a generated experiment: tracking error and minimum separation vs horizon for hard vs soft avoidance constraints (pgfplots); implementation notes (warm starting, solve time, what happens when the solver fails, running MPC per drone vs centralised); pitfalls (linearising avoidance around a stale trajectory, infeasibility from hard constraints, weights in inconsistent units, too short a horizon to stop in time); drone box (MPC as the constraint-enforcing tracker in the hybrid architecture: it follows the CBS path, respects formation and communication limits explicitly, and takes the ORCA velocity as a reference when the safety layer is active); 8-10 exercises incl. the Week-11 coding exercise (MPC that follows a path while preserving a formation or distance limit) and the derivation of the condensed QP.
