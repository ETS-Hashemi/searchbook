\section{Why this matters for a drone swarm}
\label{sec:ch06-motivation}

A drone of the swarm in \cref{ch:ch24} is flying the path that the global
planner (conflict-based search, \cbs, or its bounded-suboptimal variant
\ecbs, \cref{ch:ch09,ch:ch10}) assigned to it when an unknown quadrotor
crosses its route. The local layer (optimal reciprocal collision avoidance,
\orca, or the dynamic window approach, DWA, \cref{ch:ch13,ch:ch14}) swerves, and after two seconds the drone is three
cells off its nominal path with the intruder still between it and the next
waypoint. The replanning layer must now produce a new global path from the
current cell, and it has one control cycle to do it, say $50$~ms on a flight
computer that is also running the tracker of \cref{ch:ch18} and the attitude
controller. \dstarlite (\cref{ch:ch05}) repairs the previous search quickly
when the map changed a little and the drone moved a little; after an evasive
maneuver both changes are large, and a repair can cost as much as a fresh
search. Plain \astar (\cref{ch:ch04}) returns an optimal path, but it returns
nothing at all until it has finished, and on a $60 \times 60$ grid with a third
of the cells blocked it expands about $1\,180$ cells before it does
(\cref{sec:ch06-experiment}). If the cycle ends first, the drone has no path.

What the drone needs is a \emph{safe path now and a better one when time
permits}. Weighted \astar with $w = 3$ delivers a path after about $215$
expansions on the same grids, on average $8\,\%$ longer than optimal, which is
a perfectly good path to start flying. The question this chapter answers is
how to keep improving that path in the remaining time \emph{without throwing
the first search away}, and how to know, at every moment, how far from optimal
the current path can be. \arastar\index{ARA*} (Anytime Repairing \astar,
Likhachev, Gordon and Thrun~\cite{likhachev2003ara}) does both: it runs
weighted \astar once with a large weight, then lowers the weight step by step
and repairs the search instead of restarting it, and after every step it
publishes the current path together with a certificate $\eps'$ such that the
path costs at most $\eps'$ times the optimum. In the study plan
(\cref{ch:appA}) it is the optional second half of Week~2, next to \dstarlite:
one planner repairs the search when the \emph{map} changes, the other when the
\emph{time budget} changes.

The chapter proceeds as follows. \Cref{sec:ch06-intuition} explains the anytime
idea and why restarting weighted \astar wastes work, \cref{sec:ch06-problem}
fixes the notation, \cref{sec:ch06-algorithm} gives the pseudocode,
\cref{sec:ch06-example} runs three iterations by hand on an $8 \times 6$ grid,
\cref{sec:ch06-properties} proves the guarantees, \cref{sec:ch06-schedule}
studies the $\eps$ schedule with an experiment, \cref{sec:ch06-variants}
introduces Anytime \dstar, and \cref{sec:ch06-implementation,sec:ch06-drone}
cover the implementation and the place of the algorithm in the drone system.

\section{The idea in plain words}
\label{sec:ch06-intuition}

\subsection{A path now, a better path later}

An \textbf{anytime algorithm}\index{anytime algorithm} is one that can be
interrupted at any moment and still returns a useful answer: a first, possibly
poor, solution appears quickly, the solution improves while the algorithm keeps
running, and when it is stopped the best solution found so far is handed back.
For a planner two more things are wanted. The improvements should not be
random luck but should come with a \emph{quality certificate}, a number
$\eps'$ with the meaning ``this path costs at most $\eps'$ times the optimal
cost''; and the improvements should be cheap, so that the sequence of paths
costs little more in total than a single optimal search would have. The left
panel of \cref{fig:ch06-idea} shows the resulting picture: the cost of the best
available path decreases in steps, one step per published solution, and a
deadline that falls anywhere on the time axis finds a path ready.

  paths later; a deadline that arrives between two publications finds the last
  published path ready, together with its certificate $\eps'$. Right: the three
  lists of \arastar. States wait in \Open, are expanded once per iteration into
  \Closed, and go to \textsc{Incons} if a cheaper way to them turns up after

\subsection{Weighted \astar, and why restarting it wastes work}
\label{sec:ch06-restart}

Recall from \cref{ch:ch04} that weighted \astar\index{weighted A*} is \astar
with the key $\gcost(n) + w\,\hcost(n)$ for a weight $w \ge 1$
\cite{pohl1970heuristic}. The weight makes the search greedy: cells that look
close to the goal are expanded first, the frontier stops sweeping the whole
band $\fcost \le C^*$ and dives toward the goal instead, and the search ends
after far fewer expansions. The price is bounded: when $\hcost$ is consistent
the returned path costs at most $w \cdot C^*$ even though closed states are
never re-opened, which is the case \cref{thm:ch06-bound} proves below; with a
merely admissible $\hcost$ the bound needs re-opening
(\cref{thm:ch04-weighted}), and \cref{exr:ch06-proof} shows what breaks. On
the random grids of \cref{ch:ch04}, $w = 2$ cut the effort from $3\,324$ to
$271$ expansions on the 8-connected grid and from $756$ to $372$ on the
4-connected one, while lengthening the paths by $6$ and $12\,\%$
respectively.

The obvious anytime planner is therefore: run weighted \astar with $w = 3$,
publish the path, run it again with $w = 2$, publish, run it again with
$w = 1$, publish the optimal path. \Cref{sec:ch06-experiment} measures this
\textbf{restarted weighted \astar}\index{weighted A*!restarted} on random
$60 \times 60$ grids with the schedule $w = 3, 2.5, 2, 1.75, 1.5, 1.25, 1.1,
1$: it expands $3\,963$ cells in total, $3.4$ times as many as a single optimal
\astar, because every run starts from nothing and expands again most of the
cells that the previous run had already settled. Two kinds of work are
repeated. First, the $\gcost$-values are thrown away, although every one of
them is the cost of a real path and remains a valid upper bound. Second, most
of the states that the previous run expanded were expanded with a $\gcost$
that later runs never improve, so expanding them again produces nothing new.

\subsection{What can be reused}

\arastar keeps three things between runs: the $\gcost$-values with their
parent pointers, the frontier \Open, and a third list \textsc{Incons}
(\cref{fig:ch06-idea}, right). The key observation is that after a search only
the \emph{inconsistent} states matter: a state whose $\gcost$-value has been
lowered since it was last expanded, or that has never been expanded at all.
Expanding a consistent state again would relax the same edges with the same
value and change nothing. The inconsistent states are exactly the states in
\Open plus those that were improved \emph{after} they were expanded; weighted
\astar with re-opening would put the latter back into \Open at once, \arastar
records them in \textsc{Incons} and postpones their re-expansion to the next
iteration. So the next iteration starts from $\Open \cup \textsc{Incons}$ with
keys recomputed for the smaller $\eps$, and everything else, every consistent
state, keeps its value and costs nothing.

\begin{keyidea}
\arastar is weighted \astar with a decreasing weight $\eps$ that never
restarts. Each state is expanded at most once per iteration; a state improved
after its expansion goes to \textsc{Incons} instead of being re-expanded. When
$\eps$ decreases, \Open and \textsc{Incons} are merged and re-keyed, \Closed is
emptied, and the search continues from where it stopped. Every published path
costs at most $\eps' C^*$ with $\eps' = \min\bigl(\eps,\ \gcost(\gamma) /
\min_{n \in \Open \cup \textsc{Incons}} (\gcost(n) + \hcost(n))\bigr)$.
\end{keyidea}

\section{Problem statement and notation}
\label{sec:ch06-problem}

We use the notation of \cref{ch:ch04}: a finite directed graph $G = (V, E)$
with edge costs $c(n, n') \ge 0$, a start $s$, a goal $\gamma$, the successor
function $\Succ(n)$, the cost $\gcost^*(n)$ of a cheapest path from $s$ to $n$,
the cost $\hcost^*(n)$ of a cheapest path from $n$ to $\gamma$, the optimal
solution cost $C^* = \gcost^*(\gamma) = \hcost^*(s)$, and a heuristic
$\hcost \colon V \to \R_{\ge 0}$ that is \textbf{consistent}: $\hcost(\gamma) =
0$ and $\hcost(n) \le c(n, n') + \hcost(n')$ on every edge. Consistency is
assumed throughout the chapter; \cref{sec:ch06-properties} shows where it is
used and \cref{exr:ch06-proof} what breaks without it. During the search,
$\gcost(n)$ is the cost of the best path from $s$ to $n$ found so far
($\infty$ if none), with a parent pointer $\mathrm{parent}(n)$ that records
the predecessor on that path. On grids the nodes are cells $(x, y)$, and the
worked example uses an 8-connected grid with move costs $1$ and $\sqrt{2}$, the
corner-cutting rule of \cref{ch:ch02} and the octile distance of
\cref{ch:ch04} as $\hcost$.

\begin{definition}[Anytime search with certificates]
\label{def:ch06-anytime}
Given a heuristic search problem and a \textbf{schedule}\index{ARA*!epsilon schedule@$\eps$ schedule} of inflation factors
$\eps_1 > \eps_2 > \dots > \eps_K = 1$, an anytime planner with certificates
outputs a sequence of solution paths $P_1, P_2, \dots, P_K$ together with
numbers $\eps'_1, \eps'_2, \dots, \eps'_K$ such that $\cost(P_k) \le \eps'_k
\cdot C^*$ for every $k$ and $\eps'_K = 1$. The planner may be interrupted
after any $P_k$; the last published pair $(P_k, \eps'_k)$ is its answer.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inflated key]
\label{def:ch06-key}
For an inflation factor $\eps \ge 1$ the \textbf{inflated
key}\index{ARA*!inflated key} of a state is
\begin{equation}
  \fcost_\eps(n) = \gcost(n) + \eps\,\hcost(n).
  \label{eq:ch06-key}
\end{equation}
For $\eps = 1$ it is the $\fcost$ of \astar; for $\eps > 1$ it is the key of
weighted \astar with $w = \eps$. In \arastar the value of $\eps$ changes between
iterations, so $\fcost_\eps$ of a state in \Open must be recomputed whenever
$\eps$ does. Pearl and Kim's $\astar_\eps$ \cite{pearl1982studies} writes the
slack as $\eps$, so its bound reads $1 + \eps$; here $\eps$ is the multiplier
itself and the bound is $\eps\,C^*$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Consistent and inconsistent states]
\label{def:ch06-inconsistent}
Let $v(n)$ denote the value that $\gcost(n)$ had when $n$ was expanded for the
last time, and $v(n) = \infty$ if $n$ has never been expanded. A state $n$ with
$\gcost(n) < \infty$ is \textbf{consistent} if $v(n) = \gcost(n)$ and
\textbf{inconsistent}\index{inconsistent state} if $v(n) > \gcost(n)$, that is,
if a cheaper path to $n$ has been found since its last expansion, or it has
never been expanded. (Since $\gcost$ only decreases, $v(n) < \gcost(n)$ never
happens.) \arastar keeps the inconsistent states in two lists:
\Open\index{open set} holds those that may still be expanded in the current
iteration, and \textsc{Incons}\index{ARA*!INCONS list} holds those that were
already expanded in the current iteration. Together, $\Open \cup
\textsc{Incons}$ is exactly the set of inconsistent states.
\end{definition}

The word \emph{inconsistent} is used here in the sense of Lifelong
Planning \astar (\lpastar) and \dstarlite (\cref{ch:ch05}), where a vertex is inconsistent when its stored
$\gcost$ disagrees with the value its predecessors would give it. It is a
different notion from a consistent \emph{heuristic}; the two meet in
\cref{sec:ch06-implementation}, where inflating a consistent heuristic is what
makes states inconsistent.

\begin{definition}[Lower bound and certificate]
\label{def:ch06-certificate}
When an iteration of \arastar ends, let
\begin{equation}
  L = \min_{n \in \Open \cup \textsc{Incons}} \bigl(\gcost(n) + \hcost(n)\bigr)
  \label{eq:ch06-lower}
\end{equation}
($L = \infty$ if both lists are empty) and define the
\textbf{certificate}\index{ARA*!suboptimality certificate}
\begin{equation}
  \eps' = \min\Bigl(\eps,\ \frac{\gcost(\gamma)}{L}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:ch06-certificate}
\end{equation}
\arastar never expands $\gamma$, because the loop stops as soon as the goal
holds the smallest key, so a finite $\gcost(\gamma)$ means $\gamma \in \Open$
and therefore $L \le \gcost(\gamma) < \infty$ and $\eps' \ge 1$. Both lists
can be empty, $L = \infty$, only when $\gamma$ was never generated, which is
the failure exit on line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:fail}.
\end{definition}

\Cref{sec:ch06-properties} shows that $L \le C^*$ unless the current path is
already optimal, so $\eps'$ is a valid bound: the published path costs at most
$\eps' C^*$. In the worked example $\eps'$ is $1.65$ after the first iteration
although $\eps = 3$, and on the random grids of \cref{sec:ch06-experiment} it
is $1.41$ for $\eps = 3$. The certificate is what the drone's executive reads;
the inflation factor is only what the search was asked for.

\section{The algorithm}
\label{sec:ch06-algorithm}

\arastar consists of an inner loop \AraImprovePath, which is weighted \astar
without re-expansions (\cref{alg:ch06-improvepath}), and an outer loop
\AraMain, which runs through the schedule, does the bookkeeping between two
values of $\eps$ and publishes the paths (\cref{alg:ch06-main}). The
pseudocode follows Likhachev, Gordon and Thrun~\cite{likhachev2003ara}, with
parent pointers added so that the path can be read off.

\begin{algorithm}[htb]
\caption{\arastar, inner loop: weighted \astar without re-expansions}
\label{alg:ch06-improvepath}
\KwIn{graph $G$, goal $\gamma$, consistent heuristic $\hcost$, current $\eps$; the global tables $\gcost$, parent and the lists \Open, \Closed, \textsc{Incons}}
\KwOut{on return, $\gcost(\gamma) \le \eps\,C^*$ and the parent pointers from $\gamma$ give an $\eps$-suboptimal path}
\Fn{\AraKey{$n$}}{\KwRet $\gcost(n) + \eps\,\hcost(n)$\;}
\Fn{\AraImprovePath{}}{
  \While{$\AraKey{$\gamma$} > \min_{n \in \text{\Open}} \AraKey{$n$}$}{\label{alg:ch06-improvepath:while}
    $n \gets$ remove the state with the smallest \AraKey from \Open\label{alg:ch06-improvepath:pop}\;
    add $n$ to \Closed\label{alg:ch06-improvepath:close}\;
    \ForEach{$(n', c(n, n')) \in \Succ(n)$}{
      \If{$\gcost(n) + c(n, n') < \gcost(n')$}{\label{alg:ch06-improvepath:relax}
        $\gcost(n') \gets \gcost(n) + c(n, n')$; $\mathrm{parent}(n') \gets n$\;
        \If{$n' \notin$ \Closed}{insert $n'$ into \Open with key \AraKey{$n'$}, or update its key\label{alg:ch06-improvepath:open}}
        \Else{insert $n'$ into \textsc{Incons}\tcp*{improved after its expansion: postponed}\label{alg:ch06-improvepath:incons}}
      }
    }
  }
}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htb]
\caption{\arastar, outer loop: the schedule, the bookkeeping and the certificates}
\label{alg:ch06-main}
\KwIn{graph $G$, start $s$, goal $\gamma$, consistent heuristic $\hcost$, schedule $\eps_1 > \dots > \eps_K = 1$}
\KwOut{one path with certificate per iteration; the last one is optimal}
\Fn{\AraMain{$G$, $s$, $\gamma$, $\hcost$, $(\eps_1, \dots, \eps_K)$}}{
  $\gcost(n) \gets \infty$ for all $n$; $\gcost(s) \gets 0$; parent$(s) \gets$ \KwNil\label{alg:ch06-main:init}\;
  \Open $\gets \emptyset$; \Closed $\gets \emptyset$; \textsc{Incons} $\gets \emptyset$; $\eps \gets \eps_1$\;
  insert $s$ into \Open with key \AraKey{$s$}\;
  \For{$k \gets 1$ \KwTo $K$}{
    \If{$k > 1$}{
      $\eps \gets \eps_k$\label{alg:ch06-main:decrease}\;
      move all states of \textsc{Incons} into \Open\label{alg:ch06-main:merge}\;
      recompute the key \AraKey{$n$} of every $n \in$ \Open\label{alg:ch06-main:rekey}\;
      \Closed $\gets \emptyset$\label{alg:ch06-main:clear}\;
    }
    \AraImprovePath{}\label{alg:ch06-main:improve}\;
    \If{$\gcost(\gamma) = \infty$}{\KwRet \emph{failure}\tcp*{no path exists; \Open ran empty}\label{alg:ch06-main:fail}}
    $L \gets \min_{n \in \text{\Open} \cup \textsc{Incons}} \bigl(\gcost(n) + \hcost(n)\bigr)$\label{alg:ch06-main:bound}\;
    $\eps' \gets \min\bigl(\eps,\ \gcost(\gamma) / L\bigr)$\tcp*{the certificate of \cref{eq:ch06-certificate}}
    \AraPublish{\AraPath{parent, $\gamma$}, $\eps'$}\label{alg:ch06-main:publish}\;
    \If{$\eps' \le 1$}{\KwRet\tcp*{optimal: stop early}\label{alg:ch06-main:stop}}
  }
}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Walkthrough of \code{ImprovePath}.}
The loop condition on line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:while} replaces the goal
test of \astar. \astar stops when it \emph{pops} the goal; \AraImprovePath
stops as soon as the goal's key is no larger than the smallest key in \Open,
which is the moment at which \astar would pop it. The difference is that the
goal is never removed from \Open: it stays there with its key, so that the next
iteration, with a smaller $\eps$, can test it again. A consequence worth
remembering is that $\gamma$ is never expanded, and once it has a finite
$\gcost$ it is always in \Open.\index{ARA*!goal never expanded} Line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:pop} pops the
best state and line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:close} closes it; a closed state
is not popped again in this iteration. The relaxation on
line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:relax} is that of \astar: a strictly cheaper
path to $n'$ updates $\gcost(n')$ and the parent pointer \emph{whatever list
$n'$ is in}; only what happens next depends on the list. If $n'$ is not closed it goes
into \Open (line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:open}) with a fresh key, or its key
is updated if it is there already. If $n'$ is closed
(line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:incons}), \astar with re-opening would put it
back into \Open and expand it a second time; \arastar puts it into
\textsc{Incons} instead. Its successors keep their old, larger $\gcost$-values
for the rest of this iteration, which is exactly what makes the guarantee of
this iteration $\eps$-suboptimal rather than optimal, and what makes each
iteration cheap. \Cref{fig:ch06-incons} shows the situation in which
line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:incons} fires in the worked example.

\paragraph{Walkthrough of \code{ARAStar}.}
Lines~\ref{alg:ch06-main:init}--\ref{alg:ch06-main:improve} of the first pass
are weighted \astar with $w = \eps_1$ without re-opening, so the first
published path is exactly the path that weighted \astar would return, after
the same number of expansions. Between iterations four things happen
(lines~\ref{alg:ch06-main:decrease}--\ref{alg:ch06-main:clear}): $\eps$ is
decreased; the states of \textsc{Incons}, whose improvements have not yet been
propagated, are merged into \Open; the keys of all states in \Open are
recomputed for the new $\eps$, since a smaller $\eps$ changes the order of the
queue; and \Closed is emptied, so that every state may be expanded once more.
Nothing else is touched: the $\gcost$-values and parent pointers of all
states, including the goal, survive. The certificate on
line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:bound} is computed from the lists at the end of the
iteration and published with the path on line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:publish}.
Line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:stop} is an early exit: if the certificate already
says $1$, the rest of the schedule cannot improve anything, which happens
regularly in practice (\cref{sec:ch06-schedule}). If no path exists, the
first call of \AraImprovePath empties \Open, the loop condition
$\infty > \infty$ is false, and line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:fail} reports the
failure.

\paragraph{The published path.}
\AraPath follows the parent pointers back from $\gamma$ to $s$. This path may
be \emph{cheaper} than $\gcost(\gamma)$ says: when a state on it was improved
and sent to \textsc{Incons}, its parent pointer changed but the
$\gcost$-values of its descendants, including $\gcost(\gamma)$, were not
updated. The pointer path is always a valid path of cost at most
$\gcost(\gamma)$ (\cref{thm:ch06-pointer}), so publishing it can only help;
the certificate is still computed from $\gcost(\gamma)$, which is the quantity
the theorem bounds. \Cref{tab:ch06-differences} summarizes how one call of
\AraImprovePath differs from the \astar of \cref{ch:ch04}.

\section{A worked example}
\label{sec:ch06-example}

\begin{example}[\arastar on an $8 \times 6$ grid]
\label{ex:ch06-grid}
\Cref{fig:ch06-example} shows an 8-connected $8 \times 6$ grid with ten blocked
cells, $(0,2)$, $(0,4)$, $(2,0)$, $(2,5)$, $(3,4)$, $(4,1)$, $(5,2)$, $(6,0)$,
$(6,3)$ and $(6,4)$. The start is $s = (0,3)$, the goal $\gamma = (7,2)$;
straight moves cost $1$, diagonal moves $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$ and are
allowed only when neither cell they cut across is blocked. The heuristic is
the octile distance, so $\hcost(s) = 7 + (\sqrt{2} - 1) \cdot 1 = 7.41$, and the
schedule is $\eps = 3, 2, 1$. Ties in the key are broken toward the smaller
$\hcost$, and among equal keys the state pushed first is popped first. The
instance is \code{example\_grid()} in \code{ch06\_arastar.py}, and every number
below is produced by its self-test. The optimal cost is $C^* = 6 + 3\sqrt{2}
= 10.24$.
\end{example}

\paragraph{Second iteration, $\eps = 2$.}

is still $12.24$; the smallest key belongs to $(3,3)$ from \textsc{Incons},
$3.00 + 2 \cdot 4.41 = 11.83$, and it is expanded first. This is the postponed

\section{Properties: bounds, completeness, complexity}
\label{sec:ch06-properties}

Throughout this section $G$ is finite, the edge costs are non-negative and
$\hcost$ is consistent. The results and the proofs are those of Likhachev,
Gordon and Thrun~\cite{likhachev2003ara}, in the journal form of
\cite{likhachev2008anytime}, adapted to the notation of \cref{ch:ch04}. We
first collect the invariants that the two algorithms maintain.

\begin{lemma}[Invariants of \arastar]
\label{thm:ch06-invariants}
At every moment during \cref{alg:ch06-main}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\gcost(n)$ never increases, and whenever it is finite it is the cost
        of the path recorded by the parent pointers, so $\gcost(n) \ge
        \gcost^*(n)$;
  \item if $n$ has been expanded, with the value $v(n)$ of
        \cref{def:ch06-inconsistent}, then $\gcost(n') \le v(n) + c(n, n')$
        for every successor $n'$ of $n$;
  \item $\Open \cup \textsc{Incons}$ is the set of inconsistent states, and
        \Open and \textsc{Incons} are disjoint; a state in \textsc{Incons} was
        expanded in the current iteration.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) is line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:relax} of \cref{alg:ch06-improvepath}:
the only assignment to $\gcost(n')$ is a strict decrease to the cost of a real
path. (ii) holds at the moment $n$ is expanded, because the relaxation sets
$\gcost(n') \le \gcost(n) + c(n, n') = v(n) + c(n, n')$, and it keeps holding
because $\gcost(n')$ never increases. (iii) A state enters \Open when it is
generated ($v = \infty > \gcost$) or when its $\gcost$ decreases while it is
not closed; it enters \textsc{Incons} when its $\gcost$ decreases while it is
closed, that is, after its expansion in this iteration; in both cases it is
inconsistent. A state leaves \Open only by being expanded, which sets $v(n) =
\gcost(n)$ and makes it consistent, and leaves \textsc{Incons} only on
line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:merge} of \cref{alg:ch06-main}, into \Open.
Conversely an inconsistent state has been improved after its last expansion,
or generated and never expanded, and both events put it into one of the two
lists; nothing removes it from them except an expansion. Emptying \Closed on
line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:clear} does not change any $\gcost$ and so does not
change which states are inconsistent.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The pointer path]
\label{thm:ch06-pointer}\index{ARA*!pointer path}
If $\gcost(\gamma) < \infty$, the parent pointers from $\gamma$ lead back to
$s$ along a simple path whose cost is at most $\gcost(\gamma)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
When $\mathrm{parent}(n') \gets n$ is set, $\gcost(n') = \gcost(n) + c(n,
n')$; afterward $\gcost(n)$ can only decrease, so $\gcost(n) + c(n, n') \le
\gcost(n')$ holds from then on for every pointer, and summing along the
pointers gives a path cost of at most $\gcost(\gamma)$. The chain cannot
cycle. Suppose it closed one and look at the moment its last pointer was set,
say $\mathrm{parent}(u) \gets p$: that relaxation was strict, $\gcost(p) +
c(p,u) < \gcost(u)$ for the value $\gcost(u)$ had a moment earlier. The other
pointers of the cycle were already in place, so telescoping the inequality
above from $p$ around the cycle back to $u$ gives $\gcost(u) \le \gcost(p)$
for non-negative costs, and with the strict relaxation that forces $c(p,u) <
0$, which is impossible. So the chain ends at the only state without a parent,
$s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\eps$-suboptimality; Likhachev, Gordon and Thrun 2003]
\label{thm:ch06-bound}
Whenever \cref{alg:ch06-improvepath} selects a state $n$ for expansion,
$\gcost(n) \le \eps\,\gcost^*(n)$. When it returns with $\gcost(\gamma) <
\infty$, then $\gcost(\gamma) \le \eps\,C^*$.\index{ARA*!suboptimality theorem}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Fix one call of \AraImprovePath and consider its expansions in order. We show
the following claim by induction over that order: \emph{at any moment at which
some state $n$ satisfies $\fcost_\eps(n) \le \min_{m \in \text{\Open}}
\fcost_\eps(m)$, it holds that $\gcost(n) \le \eps\,\gcost^*(n)$}. A state
selected for expansion satisfies the premise, since it holds the minimum key;
so does $\gamma$ at the moment the loop stops, which gives the second
statement because $\gcost^*(\gamma) = C^*$.

Suppose the claim fails for $n$ and let $P = (s = n_0, n_1, \dots, n_k = n)$
be a cheapest path to $n$, so that $\gcost^*(n_{i+1}) = \gcost^*(n_i) +
c(n_i, n_{i+1})$. Let $j$ be the smallest index with $\gcost(n_j) > \eps\,
\gcost^*(n_j)$; since $\gcost(s) = 0$ we have $j \ge 1$, and $m = n_{j-1}$
satisfies $\gcost(m) \le \eps\,\gcost^*(m)$. By
\cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(iii) exactly one of three cases applies to $m$.

\emph{Case 1: $m$ is consistent}, $v(m) = \gcost(m)$. By
\cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(ii), $\gcost(n_j) \le \gcost(m) + c(m, n_j) \le
\eps\,\gcost^*(m) + c(m, n_j) \le \eps\,\bigl(\gcost^*(m) + c(m, n_j)\bigr)
= \eps\,\gcost^*(n_j)$, contradicting the choice of $j$.

\emph{Case 2: $m \in \Open$.} Consistency of $\hcost$ applied along the
segment of $P$ from $m$ to $n$, as in the proof that consistency implies
admissibility in \cref{ch:ch04}, gives $\hcost(m) \le \bigl(\gcost^*(n) -
\gcost^*(m)\bigr) + \hcost(n)$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
  \fcost_\eps(m) &= \gcost(m) + \eps\,\hcost(m)
  \le \eps\,\gcost^*(m) + \eps\,\bigl(\gcost^*(n) - \gcost^*(m)\bigr) + \eps\,\hcost(n)\\
  &= \eps\,\gcost^*(n) + \eps\,\hcost(n)
  < \gcost(n) + \eps\,\hcost(n) = \fcost_\eps(n),
\end{align*}
using $\gcost(n) > \eps\,\gcost^*(n)$ in the last step. But $m \in \Open$ and
$\fcost_\eps(n)$ is at most the smallest key in \Open: a contradiction.

\emph{Case 3: $m \in \textsc{Incons}$.} Then $m$ was expanded earlier in this
call with a value $v(m) > \gcost(m)$, and by \cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(ii)
$\gcost(n_j) \le v(m) + c(m, n_j)$. Combined with $\gcost(n_j) > \eps\,
\gcost^*(n_j) = \eps\,\gcost^*(m) + \eps\,c(m, n_j) \ge \eps\,\gcost^*(m) +
c(m, n_j)$ this gives $v(m) > \eps\,\gcost^*(m)$: when $m$ was selected for
expansion, it violated the claim. That contradicts the induction hypothesis
for that earlier expansion. (For the first expansion of the call
\textsc{Incons} is empty, so the case cannot occur and the induction has a
base.)

All three cases are contradictory, so the claim holds.
\end{proof}

The proof uses the consistency of $\hcost$ in Case~2 only, and for the
\emph{uninflated} heuristic; with a merely admissible heuristic the theorem is
false (\cref{exr:ch06-proof}). It holds for every call, whatever
$\gcost$-values were inherited from earlier iterations with larger $\eps$:
Cases~1 and~2 do not care when a value was computed.

\begin{proposition}[The certificate is valid]
\label{thm:ch06-certificate}
When \cref{alg:ch06-improvepath} returns with $\gcost(\gamma) < \infty$,
either $\gcost(\gamma) = C^*$ or $L \le C^*$, with $L$ as in
\cref{eq:ch06-lower}. In both cases $\gcost(\gamma) \le \eps' C^*$ with
$\eps'$ as in \cref{eq:ch06-certificate}, and $L \le \gcost(\gamma)$, so
$\eps' \ge 1$.\index{ARA*!suboptimality certificate}\index{lower bound!on the optimal cost}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $P = (s = n_0, \dots, n_k = \gamma)$ be an optimal path and $u$ the first
inconsistent state on $P$; it exists, because $\gamma$ is never expanded
(\cref{def:ch06-certificate}) and is therefore inconsistent as soon as
$\gcost(\gamma) < \infty$. Telescoping \cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(ii) with
$v = \gcost$ over the consistent states before $u$ gives $\gcost(u) \le
\gcost^*(u)$, hence $\gcost(u) = \gcost^*(u)$ by
\cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(i), and \cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(iii) puts
$u \in \Open \cup \textsc{Incons}$. Admissibility of $\hcost$ (implied by
consistency) gives $\gcost(u) + \hcost(u) \le \gcost^*(u) + \hcost^*(u) =
C^*$, hence $L \le C^*$. If $u = \gamma$ that reads
$\gcost(\gamma) = C^* \le \eps' C^*$; otherwise
$\gcost(\gamma) = (\gcost(\gamma) / L)\,L \le (\gcost(\gamma)/L)\,C^*$, and
$\gcost(\gamma) \le \eps\,C^*$ by \cref{thm:ch06-bound}, so the minimum of
the two factors works. Finally $\gamma$ is never expanded, so it is in
\Open with $\gcost(\gamma) + \hcost(\gamma) = \gcost(\gamma)$, and $L \le
\gcost(\gamma)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Expansions, completeness, termination]
\label{thm:ch06-properties}
Let $\eps \ge 1$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item In one call of \AraImprovePath every state is expanded at most once;
        the call performs at most $\abs{V}$ expansions and $\abs{E}$
        relaxations and terminates.\index{ARA*!expansions per iteration}
  \item For every $\eps \ge 1$, \AraImprovePath returns with $\gcost(\gamma) <
        \infty$ if and only if $\gamma$ is reachable from $s$; \arastar is
        complete.\index{ARA*!completeness}
  \item Over the iterations $\gcost(\gamma)$ never increases, and the
        iteration with $\eps = 1$ returns $\gcost(\gamma) = C^*$: the last
        published path is optimal.
  \item If, after the keys have been recomputed for a new $\eps$, the key of
        $\gamma$ is at most the smallest key in \Open, the iteration performs
        no expansion at all.\index{ARA*!zero-work iteration}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) A state is expanded only when it is popped from \Open and it is then put
into \Closed; while it is closed, an improvement sends it to \textsc{Incons},
never to \Open (line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:incons}), and \Closed is
emptied only between calls. Each expansion relaxes the out-edges of a distinct
state. (ii) If $\gamma$ is unreachable, no path to it is ever recorded and
$\gcost(\gamma)$ stays $\infty$. If it is reachable, suppose the call returns
with $\gcost(\gamma) = \infty$; the loop can only have stopped with \Open
empty. Let $u$ be the first state on a cheapest path to $\gamma$ with
$\gcost(u) = \infty$ and $p$ its predecessor on that path, which has a finite
$\gcost$ ($u \ne s$ because $\gcost(s) = 0$). If $p$ is consistent it has been
expanded, and \cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(ii) gives $\gcost(u) \le v(p) +
c(p, u) < \infty$. If $p$ is inconsistent it lies in $\Open \cup
\textsc{Incons}$ by \cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(iii); \Open is empty, so $p$
is in \textsc{Incons} and was expanded in this call, and again $\gcost(u) <
\infty$. Both contradict the choice of $u$.
(iii) is \cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(i) together with \cref{thm:ch06-bound} for
$\eps = 1$ and $\gcost(\gamma) \ge C^*$. (iv) is the loop condition on
line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:while}, which is false at once.
\end{proof}

Part~(iv) is why consecutive iterations of the experiment in
\cref{sec:ch06-experiment} can cost nothing, and part~(iii) says what the last
iteration is: plain \astar on the inconsistent states.

\paragraph{Complexity.}
With a binary heap one iteration takes $\bigO{(\abs{V} + \abs{E}) \log \abs{V}}$
time, since it performs at most $\abs{V}$ expansions and $\abs{E}$ relaxations,
like \astar (\cref{ch:ch04}); re-keying \Open between iterations is linear in
the size of \Open when the heap is rebuilt from scratch, and the certificate is
a linear scan of the lists. With $K$ inflation factors the worst case is $K$ times the cost of
\astar, and the memory is that of a single \astar: one $\gcost$-value, one
parent pointer and one list membership per touched state. The worst case is
not what happens: \cref{sec:ch06-experiment} finds a total of $1.3$ times the
work of \astar for eight iterations. What the analysis cannot promise is that
the total stays \emph{below} \astar: the first, greedy iteration expands
states with $\gcost^* + \hcost > C^*$ that \astar never touches, and the last
iteration must still expand every state with $\gcost^* + \hcost < C^*$ that
the earlier ones skipped (\cref{ch:ch04}). The anytime property has a price;
the schedule of the next section decides how small it is.\index{ARA*!complexity}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\eps$}{epsilon} schedule}

\subsection{An experiment on random grids}
\label{sec:ch06-experiment}

\astar on 25 random $60 \times 60$ grids, 8-connected with the octile
heuristic, $32\,\%$ of the cells blocked, start and goal in opposite corners,
through the schedule $\eps = 3, 2.5, 2, 1.75, 1.5, 1.25, 1.1, 1$, and records
the cost ratio to $C^*$, the certificate and the number of expansions of every
iteration; \cref{tab:ch06-experiment} and \cref{fig:ch06-anytime} show the
means. A single optimal \astar on the same instances expands $1\,177$ cells.

Four observations.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The cheap path comes first, the work is spent at the end: iteration~1
        is weighted \astar and buys a path $8.3\,\%$ above optimal, certified
        $1.41$, for $18\,\%$ of the work of \astar, while the three iterations
        with $\eps \le 1.25$ cost $1\,243$ of the $1\,541$ expansions, because
        a small $\eps$ makes the search behave like \astar and rule out every
        cell with $\gcost^* + \hcost < C^*$.
  \item Iterations~2 and~3 expand nothing: the $\eps = 3$ path already passes
        the termination test for $\eps = 2.5$ and $2$
        (\cref{thm:ch06-properties}(iv)), so they cost only the re-keying of
        \Open and the certificate stays where it was. Restarting weighted
        \astar at those $\eps$ costs $224$ and $244$ expansions and does
        improve the path, to $7\,\%$ and $5\,\%$ above optimal: \arastar
        delivers the guarantee, not the best path the effort could buy.
  \item The total is $1.3$ times one \astar for \arastar and $3.4$ times for
        the restarts. \arastar to optimality is more expensive than \astar
        alone, by $31\,\%$ here; this is the price of having had a path after
        $215$ expansions and a near-optimal one ($1.01\,C^*$, certificate
        $1.23$) after $733$.
  \item The certificate lags behind the truth: at $\eps = 1.25$ the path is
        within $1.1\,\%$ of optimal but the certificate says $22.6\,\%$; the
        gap closes only when the last iterations expand the cheap cells near
        the start and $L$ rises.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Choosing the schedule}

The schedule is the only parameter of \arastar, and \cref{tab:ch06-schedule}
summarizes what its two knobs, the initial factor $\eps_1$ and the decrement,
do. Three things the table cannot say are worth adding. Values of $\eps_1$
between $2$ and $3$ are the usual choice on grids and lattices
\cite{likhachev2003ara,likhachev2008anytime}: a greedier first search must
back out of every dead end cell by cell and is rarely faster. Small decrements
cost almost nothing, since an iteration that expands nothing costs only the
re-keying of \Open and one that expands something publishes a path; $0.2$ is a
common value. And the
certificate can drive the schedule. The inflation $\eps_{k+1}$ is only the
worst case the next iteration promises, so a value at or above the certified
$\eps'_k$ promises nothing the published path does not already deliver, and an
iteration whose re-keyed \Open leaves the goal on top expands nothing
(\cref{thm:ch06-properties}(iv)). Starting at $\eps_{k+1} = \max(1, \eps'_k -
\Delta)$ therefore skips the factors whose promise the incumbent already keeps
and makes every iteration aim at a strictly better bound
(\cref{exr:ch06-schedule} runs the rule on \cref{ex:ch06-grid}). A larger
$\eps$ can still improve the certificate in practice, as $\gcost(\gamma)$
falls and $L$ rises --- the $\eps = 2$ iteration of \cref{ex:ch06-grid} goes
from $1.65$ to $1.53$ --- so the rule trades such gains for a faster approach
to optimality. A schedule
that stops before $\eps = 1$ loses nothing the certificate does not report:
the last path still carries its own $\eps'$, which in
\cref{tab:ch06-experiment} is already $1.086$ at $\eps = 1.1$ for a path that
is in fact within $0.03\,\%$ of optimal.

\section{Variants and extensions}
\label{sec:ch06-variants}

\subsection{Anytime \dstar}
\label{sec:ch06-anytime-dstar}

\arastar repairs the search when the \emph{time budget} changes; \dstarlite
(\cref{ch:ch05}) repairs it when the \emph{edge costs} change. Anytime
\dstar\index{Anytime D*} (AD\textsuperscript{*}, Likhachev, Ferguson, Gordon,
Stentz and Thrun~\cite{likhachev2005anytime}) does both, and it is the natural
planner for a drone that must replan on a changing map within a deadline. We
describe the ideas only; the pseudocode is in the paper and in the journal
version~\cite{likhachev2008anytime}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Search direction and bookkeeping.} Like \dstarlite, AD\textsuperscript{*}
        searches backward from the goal toward the drone's current state,
        so that the moving start is the query vertex and the heuristic
        $\hcost(s_{\mathrm{current}}, \cdot)$ is measured from it. Every
        state carries the two values $\gcost$ and $\rhs$ of \cref{ch:ch05};
        a state is overconsistent if $\gcost > \rhs$ (a cheaper way has
        appeared) and underconsistent if $\gcost < \rhs$ (its way became more
        expensive).
  \item \emph{Inflated keys for improvements only.} The key of an
        overconsistent state is $[\rhs + \eps\,\hcost;\ \rhs]$, inflated as in
        \arastar; the key of an underconsistent state is $[\gcost + \hcost;\
        \gcost]$, \emph{not} inflated. Cost increases are thus propagated
        exactly and early, while improvements are propagated greedily, and
        the $\eps$-suboptimality guarantee of \cref{thm:ch06-bound} survives.
  \item \emph{The same lists.} Overconsistent states are expanded at most
        once per iteration and go to \Closed; an overconsistent state that
        is improved again after its expansion goes to \textsc{Incons}.
        Underconsistent states are handled as in \dstarlite: their $\gcost$
        is reset to $\infty$ and they and their neighbors are re-evaluated.
  \item \emph{The outer loop.} After each search the current
        $\eps$-suboptimal path is published. When edge costs change, the
        affected states are updated; if the change is large the planner
        \emph{increases} $\eps$ (or restarts with $\eps_1$) to get a new path
        quickly, if it is small it keeps $\eps$; otherwise $\eps$ is decreased
        as in \arastar. In every case \textsc{Incons} is merged into \Open,
        the keys are recomputed and \Closed is emptied before the next
        search.
\end{itemize}
The price is two mechanisms in one algorithm, with the traps of \cref{ch:ch05}
included. Static map: \arastar; changing map and a generous deadline:
\dstarlite; both: AD\textsuperscript{*}.

\subsection{Other anytime searches}

Anytime weighted \astar (Hansen and Zhou~\cite{hansen2007anytime}) takes the
opposite decision on inconsistent states: it re-opens them immediately and
keeps running weighted \astar \emph{past} its first solution, pruning every
state whose uninflated $\fcost$ is at least the cost of the best solution so
far, and reporting the ratio of that cost to the smallest uninflated $\fcost$
in \Open as its certificate. It publishes a path whenever a better one is
found rather than once per $\eps$, at the price of re-expanding states many
times, which \arastar's postponement avoids.\index{anytime weighted A*} The certificate of both algorithms is the
ratio of an incumbent solution to a lower bound, the same reasoning by which
\ecbs (\cref{ch:ch10}) bounds the suboptimality of a multi-agent plan.
\rrtstar (\cref{ch:ch17}) is anytime by construction, improving per sample
rather than per $\eps$, without a certificate.

\section{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:ch06-implementation}

The reference implementation \code{ch06\_arastar.py} follows
\cref{alg:ch06-improvepath,alg:ch06-main} on the grids of \cref{ch:ch02}. The
tables $\gcost$ and parent are dictionaries, so untouched states cost
nothing; \Closed and \textsc{Incons} are sets; and \Open is the lazy heap of
\cref{ch:ch04}, whose entries carry $(\fcost_\eps, \hcost, \mathit{counter},
n, \gcost_{\text{at push}})$. An entry is stale when its state has left
\Open or its stored $\gcost$ differs from the current one, and stale entries
are skipped when the minimum is read, so ``update its key'' on
line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:open} is simply a second push. The
tie-breaking toward the smaller $\hcost$ keeps the greedy dive of the first
iteration going and reproduces the expansion order of \cref{tab:ch06-trace}.
The heart of the code is \code{improve\_path()} in \cref{lst:ch06-improve},
a direct transcription of \cref{alg:ch06-improvepath}: the while condition
compares the goal's key with the top of the heap, the assertion documents
\cref{thm:ch06-properties}(i), and the branch on \code{t in self.closed} is
line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:incons}.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={One \code{ImprovePath} call (from \code{code/ch06\_arastar.py}). \code{fvalue} returns $\gcost + \eps\,\hcost$; \code{\_pop\_open} and \code{\_min\_open\_f} skip stale heap entries; the elided \code{trace} block records the rows of \cref{tab:ch06-trace}.},label={lst:ch06-improve}]
    def improve_path(self):
        """One ImprovePath call at the current eps; returns #expansions."""
        expansions = 0
        while self.fvalue(self.goal) > self._min_open_f():
            s = self._pop_open()
            assert s not in self.closed  # expanded at most once per call
            self.closed.add(s)
            expansions += 1
            lowered = []
            for t, c in self.grid.successors(s):
                g_new = self.g[s] + c
                if g_new < self.g.get(t, INF):
                    self.g[t] = g_new
                    self.parent[t] = s
                    if t in self.closed:
                        self.incons.add(t)       # improved but already closed
                        lowered.append((t, g_new, "INCONS"))
                    else:
                        self._push(t)            # insert or update key
                        lowered.append((t, g_new, "OPEN"))
            # (trace bookkeeping for the trace table omitted)
        return expansions
\end{lstlisting}

\Cref{lst:ch06-bound} shows the bookkeeping between iterations
(lines~\ref{alg:ch06-main:decrease}--\ref{alg:ch06-main:clear} of
\cref{alg:ch06-main}) and the certificate. Re-keying is done by rebuilding
the heap from the merged set; the sort only makes the order of equal keys
deterministic. The certificate takes $L$ over both lists and guards the
degenerate case $\gcost(\gamma) \le L$, which means that the path is
optimal.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Start of an iteration and the certificate (from \code{code/ch06\_arastar.py}).},label={lst:ch06-bound}]
    def _start_iteration(self, eps):
        """OPEN := OPEN u INCONS with keys recomputed for eps; CLOSED := {}."""
        self.eps = eps
        self.open_set |= self.incons
        self.incons = set()
        self.closed = set()
        self._heap = []
        for s in sorted(self.open_set):
            self._push(s)

    def _bound(self):
        """Return (L, eps') for the current solution.

        L = min over OPEN u INCONS of g + h is a lower bound on C*.  The
        published bound is eps' = min(eps, g(goal)/L); if g(goal) <= L the
        solution is already optimal and eps' = 1.
        """
        candidates = self.open_set | self.incons
        lower = min((self.g[s] + self.h(s) for s in candidates), default=INF)
        g_goal = self.g.get(self.goal, INF)
        ratio = 1.0 if g_goal <= lower else g_goal / lower
        return lower, max(1.0, min(self.eps, ratio))
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{The anytime interface.}
\code{ARAStar.run()} is a \python generator: it \code{yield}s a
\code{Solution} object (path, cost, $\gcost(\gamma)$, $L$, $\eps'$, expansion
counts and snapshots of the lists) after every iteration and stops when the
certificate reaches~$1$. A consumer that needs a path by a deadline simply
stops iterating; \code{anytime\_plan()} wraps this in a callback that a drone
controller would replace by ``send the path to the trajectory follower''.
Two details matter for such a consumer. The path in the \code{Solution} is
the pointer path, whose cost can be below $\gcost(\gamma)$
(\cref{thm:ch06-pointer}); the self-test checks that it never exceeds it.
And a deadline that interrupts \AraImprovePath in the middle of an iteration
is harmless: the parent pointers form a valid path at every moment, its cost
is at most the current $\gcost(\gamma)$, which is at most the value published
last, so the last certificate still holds for it. If the interrupt must be
fine-grained, check the clock every few hundred expansions inside the loop
and return; the lists are consistent between two expansions.

\paragraph{Numerics and testing.}
On an 8-connected grid $\gcost$ is a sum of $1$s and $\sqrt{2}$s, and two
routes of equal true cost can differ in the last bit; a spurious improvement
by $10^{-16}$ would then send a closed cell to \textsc{Incons} for nothing.
The self-test passes without a tolerance, but a production version should
insist on an improvement of at least a small $\eps$ in the relaxation, as
the \astar code of \cref{ch:ch04} does. The self-test runs \arastar with the
schedule $3, 2, 1.5, 1$ on 80 random $24 \times 18$ grids with a quarter of
the cells blocked, half of them 4-connected. On the 55 solvable ones it checks
against an independent \astar that every published path is valid, that $L \le
C^*$, $1 \le \eps' \le \eps$ and $\cost \le \eps' C^*$, that $\gcost(\gamma)$
never increases and that the last path is optimal with $\eps' = 1$; on the
other 25 it checks that nothing is published; and on \cref{ex:ch06-grid} it
reproduces \cref{tab:ch06-iterations}. Weighted \astar is \arastar with a
one-element schedule --- that is exactly how \code{weighted\_astar()} is
implemented, so the first iteration and the restarts of
\cref{sec:ch06-experiment} use the same code path and tie-breaking as \arastar
itself. The whole test runs in well under a second.

\begin{pitfall}[Treating the inflated key as if it were consistent]
With $\eps > 1$ the key $\gcost + \eps\,\hcost$ is not consistent even when
$\hcost$ is, and \cref{fig:ch06-incons} shows the consequence: a closed state
can receive a cheaper path later. Two wrong reactions are common. Ignoring
the improvement (``the state is closed, skip it'') leaves a wrong
$\gcost$-value and a wrong parent pointer, and the next iteration cannot
repair what it does not know; the certificate then rests on a false $L$.
Re-opening the state at once, as \astar does, keeps the bound but gives up
the ``once per iteration'' property, and on inflated keys a state can be
re-expanded many times before the iteration ends. The right move is
the one on line~\ref{alg:ch06-improvepath:incons}: record the improvement,
put the state into \textsc{Incons}, and expand it in the next iteration.
\index{ARA*!pitfalls}
\end{pitfall}

\begin{pitfall}[Forgetting \textsc{Incons}]
\textsc{Incons} looks like a bookkeeping detail, and an implementation that
drops it, or that computes $L$ over \Open only, runs and returns paths. It is
wrong in two ways. The lower bound $L$ of \cref{eq:ch06-lower} is a lower
bound on $C^*$ only because the first inconsistent state on an optimal path
is in one of the \emph{two} lists (\cref{thm:ch06-certificate}); computed over
\Open alone it can exceed $C^*$, and the certificate becomes a false promise,
up to claiming $\eps' = 1$ for a suboptimal path. And the next iteration
starts from a search in which the improved states are not in \Open: their
successors keep stale values, the search may commit to a worse route, and
even the final iteration with $\eps = 1$ can return a suboptimal path.
\Cref{exr:ch06-incons} builds a seven-node graph on which both failures
occur at once. Merging \textsc{Incons} on line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:merge} and
scanning both lists on line~\ref{alg:ch06-main:bound} are not optional.
\end{pitfall}

\begin{pitfall}[Decreasing $\eps$ too fast]
The schedule $3, 1$ is tempting: one quick path, then the optimal one. But the
second iteration is then nearly a full \astar (15 of the 22 \astar expansions
in \cref{ex:ch06-grid}), nothing is published while it runs, and a deadline
inside it leaves the drone with the greedy path, although in
\cref{tab:ch06-experiment} a path within $7\,\%$ of optimal was available for
$83$ more expansions and one within $1.1\,\%$ for $518$ more. The opposite
mistake is cheap: an iteration whose $\eps$ is still too large expands
nothing and costs one re-keying of \Open. Decrease in small steps, or use the
adaptive rule of \cref{sec:ch06-schedule}, and always keep the last published
path until the next one is complete.
\end{pitfall}

\section{Where this fits in the drone system}
\label{sec:ch06-drone}

\begin{dronebox}
\arastar belongs to the \textbf{replanning layer} of the hybrid architecture
of \cref{ch:ch24}, next to \dstarlite and \astar, and it is the planner to
call when that layer has a deadline.\index{deadline}\index{replanning layer!anytime} It receives the drone's current cell
after a local avoidance maneuver, a map in which the predicted positions of
the intruder (\cref{ch:ch18,ch:ch20}), inflated by their uncertainty, and the
reservations of the other drones are obstacles, and a time budget; it delivers
a path and a certificate $\eps'$. \Cref{fig:ch06-deadline} shows what the
three planners of this part of the book have to offer when the budget runs
out: on the grids of \cref{sec:ch06-experiment} a budget of $1\,000$
expansions leaves plain \astar without any path, restarted weighted \astar
with a path $3\,\%$ above optimal, and \arastar with a path $1\,\%$ above
optimal and a certificate of $1.23$.

Three practical rules follow from the chapter. \emph{Fly the first path:}
with $\eps_1 = 3$ it arrives after a fifth of the work of \astar, and the
later iterations can run in following control cycles while the drone moves.
\emph{Read the certificate, not $\eps$:} the executive of \cref{ch:ch24} can
commit to a path with $\eps' \le 1.2$ and spend one more cycle on one with
$\eps' = 1.6$. \emph{Search backward when the start moves.} A drone that flies while the
planner improves changes its start; the forward search of this chapter keeps
its guarantee only for the part of the path not yet flown, which is why the
anytime replanner of choice on a changing map is Anytime \dstar
(\cref{sec:ch06-anytime-dstar}), whose backward search and key modifier
absorb both the motion of the drone and the moving obstacle.

The same certificate reasoning, an incumbent divided by a lower bound,
returns at the swarm level in \ecbs (\cref{ch:ch10}). This chapter is the
optional second half of Week~2 of the study plan (\cref{ch:appA}); its coding
exercise is \cref{exr:ch06-coding}.
\end{dronebox}

\section{Summary}
\begin{summary}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An anytime planner publishes a first path quickly and better paths
        later, each with a certificate $\eps'$ such that the path costs at
        most $\eps' C^*$; a deadline finds the last published path ready.
  \item \arastar runs weighted \astar with the key $\gcost + \eps\,\hcost$
        through a schedule $\eps_1 > \dots > \eps_K = 1$ and never restarts:
        the $\gcost$-values, parent pointers and \Open survive, and the states
        improved after their expansion wait in \textsc{Incons} until the next
        iteration. Between iterations $\eps$ decreases, $\textsc{Incons}$ is
        merged into \Open, the keys are recomputed and \Closed is emptied.
  \item Each state is expanded at most once per iteration, the goal is never
        expanded, and every iteration ends with $\gcost(\gamma) \le \eps\,C^*$
        if $\hcost$ is consistent (\cref{thm:ch06-bound}); the search is
        complete for every $\eps \ge 1$ and optimal at $\eps = 1$.
  \item The certificate $\eps' = \min(\eps, \gcost(\gamma)/L)$ with $L$ the
        smallest $\gcost + \hcost$ over $\Open \cup \textsc{Incons}$ is valid
        (\cref{thm:ch06-certificate}) and usually far below $\eps$: $1.41$
        for $\eps = 3$ on random grids. The published pointer path may be
        cheaper than $\gcost(\gamma)$.
  \item On random $60 \times 60$ grids the first path costs $18\,\%$ of the
        work of \astar and is $8\,\%$ too long; optimality costs $1.3$ times
        one \astar in total, against $3.4$ times for restarted weighted
        \astar. The work is spent in the last iterations.
  \item Start with $\eps_1 = 2$--$3$ and decrease in small steps, or by the
        adaptive rule $\eps_{k+1} = \max(1, \eps'_k - \Delta)$; one big jump
        destroys the anytime behavior. Never ignore an improvement of a
        closed state, never forget \textsc{Incons}, never compute $L$ over
        \Open alone.
  \item Anytime \dstar combines \arastar with \dstarlite: inflated keys for
        overconsistent states, exact keys for underconsistent ones, a
        backward search from the goal, and an outer loop that raises $\eps$
        after large map changes and lowers it otherwise.
\end{itemize}
\end{summary}

\paragraph{Further reading.}
\arastar is due to Likhachev, Gordon and Thrun~\cite{likhachev2003ara}; the
journal article by Likhachev, Ferguson, Gordon, Stentz and
Thrun~\cite{likhachev2008anytime} contains the complete proofs of
\cref{sec:ch06-properties}, experiments on robot platforms and Anytime
\dstar, which was introduced in \cite{likhachev2005anytime} on top of
\dstarlite~\cite{koenig2002dstarlite}. Weighted \astar goes back to
Pohl~\cite{pohl1970heuristic}; Pearl~\cite{pearl1984heuristics} is the
textbook treatment of search with inflated and otherwise
``$\eps$-admissible'' heuristics, with the semi-admissible algorithms of
Pearl and Kim~\cite{pearl1982studies} as the closest ancestor of the
certificate idea, and Russell and Norvig~\cite{russell2020aima} give the
modern textbook account of weighted \astar. Hansen and
Zhou~\cite{hansen2007anytime} analyze anytime weighted \astar and compare it
with \arastar on a range of problems.

\begin{exercise}[\difficulty{1}]
\label{exr:ch06-trace}
Work with \cref{tab:ch06-trace}. (a) In step~2 of iteration~1 the expansion of
$(1,3)$ generates five cells. Compute their keys for $\eps = 3$ and for
$\eps = 1$, and explain why the diagonal neighbors are expanded first with
$\eps = 3$ although they have the larger $\gcost$. (b) At the start of
iteration~2 recompute the keys of the nine states in \Open for $\eps = 2$ and
check that $(3,3)$ has the smallest one and the goal the second smallest.
(c) Iteration~2 stops after four expansions with $\gcost(\gamma) = 12.24$
while the pointer path costs $11.41$. Derive both numbers from the parent
pointers, and list the expansions that would have been needed to make
$\gcost(\gamma)$ equal to $11.41$ within that iteration.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[\difficulty{1}]
\label{exr:ch06-bound}
At the end of iteration~1 of \cref{ex:ch06-grid} the lists are
$\Open = \{(1,1), (1,2), (1,4), (2,1), (2,4), (3,0), (4,5), (7,2)\}$ and
$\textsc{Incons} = \{(3,3)\}$, with the $\gcost$-values of
\cref{tab:ch06-trace}. (a) Compute $\gcost + \hcost$ for all nine states and
verify $L = 7.41$ and $\eps' = 1.65$. (b) Which $L$ and $\eps'$ would an
implementation publish that ignores \textsc{Incons} in
\cref{eq:ch06-lower}? (c) Show that $\hcost(s) \le \gcost(n) + \hcost(n)$ for
every state $n$ with finite $\gcost$ when $\hcost$ is consistent, conclude
that $\min(\eps, \gcost(\gamma)/\hcost(s))$ is also a valid certificate, and
explain why it is never better than \cref{eq:ch06-certificate}. Compute it
for iteration~1 and compare.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[\difficulty{2} Proof]
\label{exr:ch06-proof}
(a) Prove \cref{thm:ch06-invariants}(iii) in full, by induction over the
operations of \cref{alg:ch06-improvepath,alg:ch06-main}, and say why the
statement would fail if \Closed were \emph{not} emptied between iterations.
(b) Write out the proof of \cref{thm:ch06-certificate} for the case in which
the first inconsistent state $u$ of the optimal path is in \textsc{Incons},
and check every use of an invariant. (c) Take the graph of \cref{ch:ch04}
with an admissible but inconsistent heuristic: nodes $s, a, b, \gamma$, edges
$s \to a$ (cost $1$), $s \to b$ ($3$), $a \to b$ ($1$), $b \to \gamma$ ($2$),
and $\hcost(a) = 3$, $\hcost = 0$ elsewhere. Run \AraImprovePath with
$\eps = 1$ by hand and show that it returns $\gcost(\gamma) = 5 > C^* = 4$
and publishes the certificate $\eps' = 1$; note whether that certificate is
about $\gcost(\gamma)$ or about the cost of the path the pointers record.
Which case of the proof of
\cref{thm:ch06-bound} breaks, and which inequality does that case need?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[\difficulty{2}]
\label{exr:ch06-incons}
Consider the graph with nodes $s, a, b, c, d, e, \gamma$ and the edges
$s \to a$ (cost $1$), $s \to b$ ($1$), $s \to d$ ($2$), $a \to c$ ($1$),
$b \to c$ ($2$), $c \to e$ ($1$), $c \to \gamma$ ($5$), $d \to \gamma$ ($3.5$),
$e \to \gamma$ ($2$), and the heuristic $\hcost(s) = 2$, $\hcost(a) = 2$,
$\hcost(b) = 1$, $\hcost(c) = 1$, $\hcost(d) = 2$, $\hcost(e) = 2$,
$\hcost(\gamma) = 0$. (a) Check that $\hcost$ is consistent and determine
$C^*$. (b) Run \arastar with the schedule $3, 1$ by hand: for each iteration
give the expansions in order, the contents of \textsc{Incons}, the published
pointer path with its cost, $\gcost(\gamma)$, $L$ and $\eps'$. (c) Repeat
the second iteration with \textsc{Incons} discarded at the end of the first:
which path and which certificate are published, and what is wrong with
each? (d) Explain why an implementation that re-opens $c$ immediately would
also be correct on this graph, and what it costs in general.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[\difficulty{2}]
\label{exr:ch06-schedule}
Use \cref{tab:ch06-experiment}. (a) For every iteration compute the decrease
of the mean cost ratio per $100$ expansions, for \arastar and for the
restarts; in which iterations is improvement cheapest? (b) A replanning cycle
allows $800$ expansions. Which iteration of the schedule is complete when the
cycle ends, what are the published cost and certificate, and what do restarted
weighted \astar and plain \astar deliver at that moment? (c) Explain with
\cref{thm:ch06-properties}(iv) why iterations~2 and~3 expand nothing, and why
the certificate nevertheless stays at $1.41$ instead of falling to $2.5$ or
$2$ --- or rising to them. (d) Apply the adaptive rule $\eps_{k+1} = \max(1,
\eps'_k - 0.2)$ to \cref{ex:ch06-grid}, starting from $\eps_1 = 3$: which
schedule results, and, running \code{ARAStar} with it, how many expansions
does each iteration cost and when does the optimal path appear?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[\difficulty{3} Coding]
\label{exr:ch06-coding}
This is the optional coding exercise of Week~2 of the study plan
(\cref{ch:appA}). Implement \arastar yourself, in the API of
\code{code/ch06\_arastar.py}, and do not read that file until you are stuck.
(a) Write \code{ARAStar(grid, start, goal, schedule).run()} as a generator
that yields, after every iteration, the pointer path, its cost,
$\gcost(\gamma)$, $L$, $\eps'$ and the expansion count, with a lazy heap, the
sets \Closed and \textsc{Incons}, and the re-keying of \Open between
iterations. Reproduce \cref{tab:ch06-iterations} and the first ten rows of
\cref{tab:ch06-trace}. (b) On 50 random $40 \times 40$ grids, check every
published path against an independent \astar: valid path, cost $\le \eps'
C^*$, $L \le C^*$, last path optimal. (c) Plot the cost ratio and the
certificate against \emph{wall-clock time} and against cumulative expansions
for the schedules $(3, 2, 1)$, $(3, 2.5, \dots, 1)$ and $(5, 4, \dots, 1)$,
and add restarted weighted \astar and plain \astar; mark the first-solution
times. (d) Add a deadline: \code{run(budget\_s=0.02)} must return the best
path available after $20$~ms, interrupting an iteration if necessary, and
never a path worse than the last published one.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[\difficulty{3}]
\label{exr:ch06-deadline}
Anytime replanning under a deadline. The replanning layer of \cref{ch:ch24}
runs on a flight computer that manages about $20\,000$ expansions per
second, and a control cycle lasts $50$~ms. (a) How many expansions fit into a
cycle? On maps like those of \cref{tab:ch06-experiment}, which iteration
finishes within the cycle, and what do \arastar, the restarts and plain
\astar deliver? Repeat for a $20$~ms cycle. (b) If the deadline falls inside
an iteration, argue from \cref{thm:ch06-pointer} and \cref{thm:ch06-bound}
that the pointer path is then a valid path within the previous iteration's
certificate. (c) After iteration~2 of \cref{ex:ch06-grid} the intruder blocks
the cell $(4,3)$. Which cells now have an invalid parent pointer or
$\gcost$-value, that is, are underconsistent in the sense of \cref{ch:ch05}? What would Anytime \dstar do
with them and with $\eps$, and what is the best \arastar alone can do?
(d) Propose a rule for the replanning layer that chooses between
``keep improving'', ``raise $\eps$ and repair'' and ``restart'' from the
number of changed edges, the certificate and the time left in the cycle.
\end{exercise}
