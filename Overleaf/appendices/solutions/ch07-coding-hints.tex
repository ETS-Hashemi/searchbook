\begin{solution}{exr:ch07-coding}
Checkpoints. Write your own first, then compare with \code{validate\_\allowbreak plan},
\code{first\_\allowbreak conflict} and \code{all\_\allowbreak conflicts} (\cref{alg:ch07-pairwise}), their hashed
twins (\cref{alg:ch07-hashed}) and the parsers in \code{ch07\_\allowbreak mapf.py}. A path is a list
of cells indexed by time, and the one function \code{position(path, t)}, which returns the
last cell beyond the arrival, encodes the parking rule of \cref{def:ch07-path}; the hashed
detector needs, per time step, a dictionary from cell to agent and one from the ordered
pair $(u,v)$ to agent. Steps: write \code{position} and test it past the end of a path;
scan $t=0,\dots,H$ with the horizon of \cref{def:ch07-plan}, emitting records that carry
both agents, the cell or edge and the time; rewrite with the two dictionaries and assert
that both detectors agree on thousands of random plans; add the parsers, keeping in mind
that a \code{.scen} line stores $x$ before $y$. Expected numbers: on \cref{ex:ch07-grid}
the naive plan has exactly three conflicts and the conflict-free plan has $\sumcost=12$,
$\makespan=5$. On random $32\times32$ maps with $20\,\%$ obstacles the naive plan carries
about $1$ conflict at $k=10$, $50$ at $k=40$ and $260$ at $k=100$ --- quadratic, the shape
of \cref{fig:ch07-conflict-growth} --- and the pairwise detector needs $0.8$ and $120$~ms
against $0.2$ and $3.4$~ms for the hashed one. Classic mistakes: comparing positions only,
so swaps vanish; indexing a move by its arrival instead of its departure; ending the scan
at the shorter path. Done when the checker finds every planted conflict of
\cref{exr:ch07-conflict-cases} and nothing else --- half of the Week~3 milestone
(\cref{ch:appA}). Keep it: it is the \cbs detector of Week~4 and the capstone's re-check.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch07-prioritized}
Checkpoints. This is a throwaway \cref{ch:ch08}: one space-time \astar per agent
(\cref{ch:ch04}) against the paths already planned, no reservation table yet. Reuse your
search and your detector from \cref{exr:ch07-coding}; \code{prioritized\_\allowbreak planning} in
\code{ch08\_\allowbreak prioritized.py} is the reference to read afterwards. Steps: after planning
agent~$i$, give every later agent a vertex constraint $\langle v,t\rangle$ per occupied
cell, a constraint on the \emph{reversed} edge $\langle v,u,t\rangle$ per move $u\to v$
(this, and only this, forbids swaps) and a permanent block of its goal from its arrival
on; give the low level a horizon, or it loops forever on an agent that cannot get through;
validate every plan before you believe any cost. Expected numbers: on \cref{ex:ch07-grid}
the index order returns $\sumcost=17$ against the optimum $12$, only the order
$(a_2,a_1,a_3)$ reaches $12$, and four of the six orders fail outright --- an early sight
of \cref{thm:ch08-incomplete} and \cref{thm:ch08-suboptimal}. On the corridor of
\cref{exr:ch07-corridor} with $n=4$ and the pocket at $(1,1)$, planning the left agent
first fails and planning the right agent first succeeds with costs $5$ and $3$,
$\sumcost=8$, which is optimal. Classic mistakes: forgetting the parked goal, so a later
agent plans through an arrived one; forbidding the same edge instead of the reversed one;
raising an exception on failure instead of returning a value, which hides how often the
planner gives up. Done when you own one instance that one order solves and another does
not, and validator and planner agree on every run.
\end{solution}
