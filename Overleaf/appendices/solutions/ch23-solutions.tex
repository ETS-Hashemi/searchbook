\begin{solution}{exr:ch23-laplacian}
With the edges $\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{3,4\},\{4,1\},\{1,3\}$ the degrees are $(3,2,3,2)$ and
\[
\mat{L}=\begin{pmatrix} 3&-1&-1&-1\\ -1&2&-1&0\\ -1&-1&3&-1\\ -1&0&-1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every row sums to zero, so $\mat{L}\vect{1}=\vect{0}$. For $\vect{x}=(1,0,0,1)\T$ the quadratic form is $\vect{x}\T\mat{L}\vect{x}=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}(x_i-x_j)^2=1+0+1+0+1=3$ (the edges $\{1,2\}$, $\{3,4\}$ and $\{1,3\}$ each contribute $1$). Guess eigenvectors orthogonal to $\vect{1}$: $\mat{L}(0,1,0,-1)\T=2\,(0,1,0,-1)\T$, $\mat{L}(1,0,-1,0)\T=4\,(1,0,-1,0)\T$ and $\mat{L}(1,-1,1,-1)\T=4\,(1,-1,1,-1)\T$, so the spectrum is $\{0,2,4,4\}$ (check: the trace is $10$). Hence $\lambda_2=2$, the time constant is $1/\lambda_2=0.5$~s, the safe step size is $\eps<1/d_{\max}=1/3$ and the exact bound is $\eps<2/\lambda_4=1/2$. A useful shortcut: the complement of the graph in $K_4$ is the single edge $\{2,4\}$ with Laplacian spectrum $\{0,0,0,2\}$, and $\mat{L}(G)+\mat{L}(\bar G)=4\mat{I}-\vect{1}\vect{1}\T$ gives the non-zero eigenvalues of $G$ as $4-\{2,0,0\}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch23-average}
(a) $\vect{1}\T\vect{x}_{k+1}=\vect{1}\T(\mat{I}-\eps\mat{L})\vect{x}_k=\vect{1}\T\vect{x}_k$ because $\vect{1}\T\mat{L}=\vect{0}\T$, so the sum and hence the average is the same at every step, for every $\eps$. Row $i$ of the update reads $x_{i,k+1}=(1-\eps d_i)x_{i,k}+\eps\sum_j a_{ij}x_{j,k}$: the weights are non-negative when $\eps\le 1/d_{\max}$ and they sum to $1$, so $x_{i,k+1}$ is a convex combination of states at step $k$ and cannot exceed $\max_j x_{j,k}$ (nor fall below the minimum). (b) With $R_{\mathrm{comm}}=2$~m the graph has the two components $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4\}$, each of which runs its own consensus: the limit is $(2,2,12,12)$, the two cluster averages. The global average is $28/4=7$, so $\vect{\delta}(\infty)=(-5,-5,5,5)\T$ and $\norm{\vect{\delta}}=10$ for ever; $\lambda_2=0$ is the only local symptom. (c) Write $\vect{u}_i=\vect{y}_i-\bar{\vect{y}}$, so $\sum_i\vect{u}_i=\vect{0}$. Then $\sum_{i<j}\norm{\vect{y}_i-\vect{y}_j}^2=\tfrac12\sum_i\sum_j\norm{\vect{u}_i-\vect{u}_j}^2=\tfrac12\bigl(n\sum_i\norm{\vect{u}_i}^2+n\sum_j\norm{\vect{u}_j}^2-2\norm{\sum_i\vect{u}_i}^2\bigr)=n\sum_i\norm{\vect{u}_i}^2$. For the complete formation graph $\abs{E_F}=n(n-1)/2$, so $e_F^2=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\cdot n\sum_i\norm{\vect{u}_i}^2=\frac{2n}{n-1}e_c^2$ using $e_c^2=\frac1n\sum_i\norm{\vect{u}_i}^2$. For \cref{ex:ch23-square}, $n=4$ gives $e_F=\sqrt{8/3}\,e_c=1.633\times1.794=2.930$~m over all six pairs, larger than the $2.500$~m of the four edges of the communication graph, which omits the two pairs $\{1,4\}$ and $\{2,4\}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch23-proof}
Write $\mat{P}=\mat{I}-\eps\mat{L}$. It is symmetric, and if $\mat{L}\vect{v}_k=\lambda_k\vect{v}_k$ then $\mat{P}\vect{v}_k=(1-\eps\lambda_k)\vect{v}_k$, so $\mat{P}$ has the eigenvalues $\mu_k=1-\eps\lambda_k$ with the same orthonormal eigenvectors. Because $\vect{1}\T\mat{L}=\vect{0}\T$ we have $\vect{1}\T\vect{x}_{k+1}=\vect{1}\T\vect{x}_k$: the average $\bar x$ is preserved. Let $\vect{\delta}_k=\vect{x}_k-\bar x\vect{1}$; then $\vect{\delta}_{k+1}=\mat{P}\vect{\delta}_k$ and $\vect{\delta}_k\perp\vect{1}$ for all $k$. Expanding $\vect{\delta}_0=\sum_{k\ge2}c_k\vect{v}_k$ gives $\vect{\delta}_m=\sum_{k\ge2}c_k\mu_k^m\vect{v}_k$ and, by orthonormality, $\norm{\vect{\delta}_m}\le\rho^m\norm{\vect{\delta}_0}$ with $\rho=\max_{k\ge2}\abs{1-\eps\lambda_k}=\max(\abs{1-\eps\lambda_2},\abs{1-\eps\lambda_n})$. It remains to show $\rho<1$: since the graph is connected, $\lambda_2>0$, and $\eps>0$ gives $1-\eps\lambda_k<1$ for $k\ge2$; since $\lambda_n\le 2d_{\max}$ (Gershgorin: every eigenvalue of $\mat{L}$ lies in a disc centred at some $d_i$ with radius $d_i$) and $\eps<1/d_{\max}$, we get $1-\eps\lambda_k\ge1-\eps\lambda_n>1-2=-1$. So every $\mu_k$, $k\ge2$, lies strictly inside $(-1,1)$, $\rho<1$, and $\vect{x}_m\to\bar x\vect{1}$ geometrically. The exact condition is $\eps<2/\lambda_n$; the bound $1/d_{\max}$ only uses information every drone has locally.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch23-directed}
(a) With $a_{ij}=1$ when $i$ hears $j$, $a_{21}=a_{32}=1$ and $\mat{D}=\diag(0,1,1)$, so $\dot x_1=0$, $\dot x_2=x_1-x_2$, $\dot x_3=x_2-x_3$. Drone~1 is a root that reaches 2 and 3, so the digraph has a directed spanning tree. Integrating, $x_1\equiv x_1(0)$, $x_2(t)=x_1(0)+(x_2(0)-x_1(0))e^{-t}$ and $x_3(t)=x_1(0)+(x_3(0)-x_1(0))e^{-t}+(x_2(0)-x_1(0))\,t\,e^{-t}$: every state converges to $x_1(0)$. Solving $\vect{w}\T\mat{L}=\vect{0}\T$ with $\vect{w}\T\vect{1}=1$ gives $\vect{w}=(1,0,0)\T$, which is exactly ``the value of the root''. (b) Adding ``drone~1 hears drone~3'' makes the digraph the directed cycle $1\leftarrow3\leftarrow2\leftarrow1$; every drone has in-degree and out-degree $1$, so the digraph is balanced, $\vect{1}\T\mat{L}=\vect{0}\T$, the sum is conserved and $\vect{w}=\vect{1}/3$: the states converge to the average. (c) With only $a_{21}=a_{23}=1$, drones~1 and~3 have no incoming edge, so neither can be reached from any root and there is no directed spanning tree. Here $\dot x_1=\dot x_3=0$ and $\dot x_2=x_1+x_3-2x_2$, so the limits are $x_1(0)$, $\tfrac12(x_1(0)+x_3(0))$ and $x_3(0)$: consensus only if $x_1(0)=x_3(0)$. No protocol can do better, because drones~1 and~3 receive nothing at all and can therefore never learn of each other.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch23-inconsistent}
(a) Summing the protocol over $i$ gives $\sum_i\dot{\pos}_i=\sum_i\sum_j a_{ij}(\pos_j-\pos_i)-\sum_i\sum_j a_{ij}\vect{d}_{ij}$. The first double sum vanishes because the graph is undirected (each pair contributes $(\pos_j-\pos_i)+(\pos_i-\pos_j)$). In general, therefore, the centroid moves with the constant velocity $-\frac1n\sum_i\sum_{j\in N_i}\vect{d}_{ij}$ and the swarm drifts away for ever, even though the relative positions settle. That velocity vanishes whenever $\vect{d}_{ji}=-\vect{d}_{ij}$ on every edge, because the two terms of each edge then cancel in pairs; edgewise antisymmetry is sufficient but not necessary, since only the total sum has to vanish. On the path $1-2-3$ with $\vect{d}_{12}=\vect{d}_{21}=(1,0)\T$ and $\vect{d}_{23}=\vect{d}_{32}=(-1,0)\T$ the four terms cancel across the two edges and the centroid stands still, although no edge is antisymmetric. (b) With antisymmetric $\vect{d}_{ij}$ the protocol is $\dot{\pos}_i=-\nabla_{\pos_i}J$ for $J(\pos)=\tfrac12\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}\norm{\pos_j-\pos_i-\vect{d}_{ij}}^2$ (differentiate one term with respect to $\pos_i$ to check). $J$ is a convex quadratic bounded below, so gradient descent converges to a minimiser and $J$ decreases monotonically. If the vectors close around every cycle, $J_{\min}=0$ and the minimisers are the translated formations; otherwise $J_{\min}>0$ and the swarm settles into a least-squares compromise with residual formation error $e_F=\sqrt{2J_{\min}/\abs{E}}$. For the triangle with $\vect{d}_{12}=\vect{d}_{23}=(1,0)\T$ and $\vect{d}_{31}=(-1,0)\T$ the mismatch $(1,0)\T$ is spread evenly, each edge ends with error $(1/3,0)\T$ and $e_F=1/3$~m. (c) Storing offsets $\vect{o}_i$ and computing $\vect{d}_{ij}=\vect{o}_j-\vect{o}_i$ makes both antisymmetry and cycle closure automatic.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch23-range}
(a) $(\pos_i,\pos_j)\mapsto\pos_i-\pos_j$ is linear and the ball $\norm{\vect{z}}\le R_{\mathrm{comm}}$ is convex, and the preimage of a convex set under a linear map is convex. In the MPC of \cref{ch:ch21} the constraint is $\norm{\pos_i(h)-\pos_j(h)}\le R_{\mathrm{comm}}$ for every kept edge $\{i,j\}\in E_{\mathrm{keep}}$ and every step $h=1,\dots,H$ of the horizon: a second-order cone in the decision variables. (b) The $Q$ inequalities describe the regular $Q$-gon whose facets are tangent to the circle of radius $R_{\mathrm{comm}}\cos(\pi/Q)$; its circumradius is $R_{\mathrm{comm}}\cos(\pi/Q)/\cos(\pi/Q)=R_{\mathrm{comm}}$, so the polygon is inscribed in the disc and every plan that satisfies the inequalities keeps $\norm{\pos_i-\pos_j}\le R_{\mathrm{comm}}$. The price is paid in the facet directions, where only $R_{\mathrm{comm}}\cos(\pi/Q)$ of the range is usable: for $Q=8$ that is $1-\cos(\pi/8)=7.6\%$, or $0.24$~m of a $3.2$~m radio. (c) For $\ell_{\mathrm{act}}<\ell<R_{\mathrm{comm}}$, $V_{\mathrm{range}}'(\ell)=\frac{(\ell-\ell_{\mathrm{act}})\bigl[2(R_{\mathrm{comm}}-\ell)+(\ell-\ell_{\mathrm{act}})\bigr]}{(R_{\mathrm{comm}}-\ell)^2}$, which tends to $0$ as $\ell\downarrow\ell_{\mathrm{act}}$ and to $+\infty$ as $\ell\uparrow R_{\mathrm{comm}}$. The velocity $-V'(\ell)$ therefore matches the value $0$ of the inactive branch: the term switches on continuously, with no jump in the command. (d) $\lambda_2$ is one number for the whole graph: it falls when \emph{some} cut is about to open, but it does not say which edge, and an edge of $E_{\mathrm{keep}}$ can be at $0.99R_{\mathrm{comm}}$ while $\lambda_2$ is still comfortable because other edges hold the graph together. Log the individual lengths $\ell_{ij}$ of the kept edges, or the margin $\min_{\{i,j\}\in E_{\mathrm{keep}}}(R_{\mathrm{comm}}-\ell_{ij})$, alongside $\lambda_2$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch23-secondorder}
(a) In the eigenbasis of $\mat{L}$ the mode with eigenvalue $\lambda$ obeys $\ddot q+(k_v\lambda+k_d)\dot q+k_p\lambda q=0$, with characteristic polynomial $s^2+(k_v\lambda+k_d)s+k_p\lambda$. A quadratic with positive coefficients has both roots in the open left half-plane, so every mode with $\lambda>0$ decays if $k_p>0$ and $k_v\lambda+k_d>0$; the mode $\lambda=0$ gives $s\in\{0,-k_d\}$. With $k_v=k_d=0$ the roots are $s=\pm\mathrm{i}\sqrt{k_p\lambda}$: undamped oscillation at the angular frequencies $\sqrt{k_p\lambda_k}$, which for the example graph and $k_p=1$ are $1$, $\sqrt3$ and $2$~rad/s. For $k_d=0$ the damping ratio of mode $\lambda$ is $\zeta=\tfrac12 k_v\sqrt{\lambda/k_p}$; choosing $k_v=2\sqrt{k_p/\lambda_2}$ makes the slowest mode critically damped and the faster ones overdamped. (b) Without feed-forward the error $\vect{y}=\pos-\vect{o}-\vect{r}(t)$ of the first-order leader-follower controller obeys $\dot{\vect{y}}=-(k_c\mat{L}+k_l\mat{B})\vect{y}-\dot{\vect{r}}\vect{1}$, so with $\dot{\vect{r}}=v\,\vect{e}$ constant the steady state is $\vect{y}_{ss}=-v\,(k_c\mat{L}+k_l\mat{B})\inv\vect{1}\otimes\vect{e}$. For the example graph, $k_c=k_l=1$ and drone~1 pinned, solving $(\mat{L}+\mat{B})\vect{z}=\vect{1}$ gives $\vect{z}=(4,\,16/3,\,17/3,\,20/3)\T$: at $v=1$~m/s the leader trails its reference by $4$~m and the tail drone by $6.67$~m, and the edge errors $z_j-z_i$ give $e_F=\sqrt{(16/9+25/9+1/9+1)/4}\approx1.19$~m for ever. Adding $\dot{\vect{r}}$ to every drone's command removes the forcing term and the lag.
\end{solution}
