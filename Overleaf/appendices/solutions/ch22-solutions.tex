\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-lp}
The vertices are $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$, $(4,1.5)$, $(2.5,3.75)$ and $(0,2.5)$
with objective values $0$, $4$, $7$, $10$ and $5$; the LP optimum is
$(2.5,3.75)$ with value $10$. Branching on $x$ first: the child $x \le 2$
has the LP optimum $(2,3.5)$ with bound $9$ (at $x = 2$ the row $-x + 2y
\le 5$ gives $y \le 3.5$); the child $x \ge 3$ has the LP optimum $(3,3)$
with value $9$, which is integral and becomes the incumbent. The first
child's bound $9$ is not better than the incumbent, so it is pruned: three
nodes. Branching on $y$ first: the child $y \le 3$ has the optimum $(3,3)$,
value $9$, integral; the child $y \ge 4$ needs $x \ge 3$ from $-x + 2y \le
5$ and $x \le 7/3$ from $3x + 2y \le 15$, so it is infeasible: again three
nodes. Both trees prove that the integer optimum is $9$, one below the LP
bound $10$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-tightm}
The block of \cref{ex:ch22-two} is $[4,6] \times [1.5,5.5]$ inside the
workspace $W = [0,10] \times [0,8]$, so \cref{eq:ch22-tightM} gives
$M_1 = X_{\max} - x_{\min} = 6$~m, $M_2 = x_{\max} - X_{\min} = 6$~m,
$M_3 = Y_{\max} - y_{\min} = 6.5$~m and $M_4 = y_{\max} - Y_{\min} =
5.5$~m. For the separation rows \cref{eq:ch22-sep} with $d_{\min} = 1$~m,
$M_x = d_{\min} + (X_{\max} - X_{\min}) = 11$~m and
$M_y = d_{\min} + (Y_{\max} - Y_{\min}) = 9$~m.

Too small an $M$ cuts off legal positions. Take $M_1 = 3$ instead of $6$
and the point $\pos_k = (9, 6.5)$, which is inside $W$ and above the block,
hence legal. The first row reads $x_k \le 4 + 3 b_{k,1}$: with
$b_{k,1} = 0$ it demands $x_k \le 4$, so $b_{k,1} = 1$ is forced, and then
it demands $x_k \le 7 < 9$. No choice of binaries admits the point, so a
feasible position has become infeasible. With the tight $M_1 = 6$ the
switched-off row reads $x_k \le 10$ and holds everywhere in $W$, which is
all it should do.

With $M_1 = 0$ the first row is $x_k \le x_{\min} = 4$ whatever $b_{k,1}$
is: the row can no longer be switched off, and the drone is pinned to the
strip left of the block at every step. The model of \cref{ex:ch22-two} is
then infeasible, because drone~A must reach $x = 9$ and drone~B starts
there.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-separation}
Apply \cref{prop:ch22-bigm} to the square
$S = [x^j_k - d_{\min}, x^j_k + d_{\min}] \times [y^j_k - d_{\min},
y^j_k + d_{\min}]$ of side $2 d_{\min}$ centred on drone $j$: the
condition $\norm{\pos^i_k - \pos^j_k}_\infty \ge d_{\min}$ says exactly
that $\pos^i_k$ lies outside $S$, and \cref{eq:ch22-sep} is
\cref{eq:ch22-bigm} for that square, written with the differences
$x^i_k - x^j_k$ and $y^i_k - y^j_k$ so that every row stays linear in both
positions. If binaries satisfy \cref{eq:ch22-sep}, the sum row forces some
$c^{ij}_{k,q} = 0$ and the corresponding side condition of
\cref{eq:ch22-sep-sides} holds, so the drones are separated. Conversely,
if one side condition holds, set its binary to $0$ and the other three to
$1$; those rows then read $x^i_k - x^j_k \ge d_{\min} - M_x$ and
$y^i_k - y^j_k \ge d_{\min} - M_y$, which hold for every pair of positions
in $W$ because $M_x$ and $M_y$ are the largest violations possible inside
$W$. The square contains the disc of radius $d_{\min}$, so the encoding is
conservative, never optimistic.

A convex polygon $P = \set{\vect{p} : \vect{a}_l^\top \vect{p} \le c_l,\;
l = 1, \dots, r}$ with $r$ edges needs $r$ binaries per step,
$\vect{a}_l^\top \pos_k \ge c_l - M_l b_{k,l}$ with
$M_l = c_l - \min_{\vect{p} \in W} \vect{a}_l^\top \vect{p}$, and
$\sum_{l=1}^{r} b_{k,l} \le r - 1$: outside a convex set means on the far
side of at least one of its supporting half-planes. A box in three
dimensions is the same with $r = 6$ faces, six binaries and
$\sum_j b_{k,j} \le 5$.

Four drones, two hexagonal obstacles and $N = 20$ therefore need
$6 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 \cdot 20 = 960$ obstacle binaries and
$4 \binom{4}{2} \cdot 20 = 480$ separation binaries, $1\,440$ in all --
about three times the $480$ of four drones with one rectangle, and the
reason a polygonal approximation of a round obstacle is expensive.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-margin}
With $v_{\max} = 3$~m/s and $\dt = 0.5$~s, $\delta = v_{\max}\dt/2 =
0.75$~m and the sampled separation must be $d_{\min} + v_{\max}\dt =
2.5$~m. The inflation must be applied on all four sides because the
proposition bounds the $\infty$-distance to the nearest sample in every
direction, and a drone skimming the top edge can dip below it between two
samples just as one passing the left edge can drift right. The self-test
of \code{ch22\_milp.py} solves the safe instance: fuel $16.78$~m/s instead
of $12.02$ (a price of $40\,\%$ for a guarantee), about $2.6$~s and $48$
nodes, no penetration of the original block between samples and a
continuous-time minimum separation of $4.08$~m, far above the required
$1$~m because the margins assume top speed in the worst direction at every
step. Inflating the block only, with $d_{\min} = 1$~m at the samples, gives
a plan whose separation between samples drops to $0.06$~m: both margins
are needed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-time}
Let $k_i$ be the step with $w^i_{k_i} = 1$; the choice row
$\sum_k w^i_k = 1$ makes it unique. For $k \ge k_i$ the sum
$\sum_{k' \le k} w^i_{k'}$ equals $1$, the right-hand side of the last row
of \cref{eq:ch22-time} is $M_{\mathrm{g}} \cdot 0 = 0$, and
$\abs{p^i_{k,d} - p^i_{\mathrm{g},d}} \le 0$ forces $\pos^i_k =
\pos^i_{\mathrm{g}}$ in both coordinates: the drone is at its goal at every
sampled instant from its arrival step on. For $k < k_i$ the sum is $0$, the
right-hand side is $M_{\mathrm{g}}$, and with $M_{\mathrm{g}}$ the
workspace diameter the row holds for every position in $W$: it is vacuous.

Two plans can share the arrival step and differ in fuel. In
\cref{ex:ch22-two} both drones can arrive at $k = 9$ by accelerating hard
for one step, coasting and decelerating hard once, or by wandering left and
right first and still arriving at $k = 9$; $J_{\mathrm{time}}$ is
identical, $J_{\mathrm{fuel}}$ is not, and the solver may return either.
The tie-breaker $10^{-3} J_{\mathrm{fuel}}$ picks the cheaper one and makes
the optimum well defined. The multiple must be small enough never to buy an
arrival step: one step costs $\dt = 0.5$~s of $J_{\mathrm{time}}$ while
$10^{-3} J_{\mathrm{fuel}}$ is a few hundredths of a second's worth here.

A time window $[t^i_{\mathrm{a}}, t^i_{\mathrm{b}}]$ needs no new variable,
only a narrower choice row,
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{k \,:\, k\dt \in [t^i_{\mathrm{a}}, t^i_{\mathrm{b}}]} w^i_k = 1,
\end{equation*}
which sets every binary outside the window to zero. Forbidding two drones
in one arrival step is the ``at most one'' gadget of
\cref{tab:ch22-gadgets}, $\sum_i w^i_k \le 1$ for every $k$, one row per
step (feasible only when $N \ge m$).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-schedule}
Let $z_{i,p,q} \in \set{0,1}$ mean ``drone $i$ lands on pad $p$ in slot
$q$'', with $p = 1, 2$ and $q = 1, \dots, Q$; slot $q$ ends at $30q$
seconds, which is also the waiting time it charges. The program is
\begin{align*}
  \min \; & \sum_{i} \sum_{p} \sum_{q} 30\, q\, z_{i,p,q}
    && \text{total waiting time},\\
  & \sum_{p} \sum_{q} z_{i,p,q} = 1 && \text{for every drone } i,\\
  & \sum_{i} z_{i,p,q} \le 1 && \text{for every pad } p \text{ and slot } q,\\
  & \sum_{i} \bigl(z_{i,p,q} + z_{i,p,q+1}\bigr) \le 1
    && \text{for every pad } p,\; q = 1, \dots, Q-1,\\
  & z_{i,p,q} = 0 && \text{whenever } 30 q > T_i.
\end{align*}
The first family is the choice gadget of \cref{tab:ch22-gadgets} (each
drone lands exactly once), the second and third are the at-most-one and
assignment gadgets: the third forbids two landings on one pad in the same
or in adjacent slots, which is the ``at least two slots apart'' rule, and
it implies the second for every slot that has a neighbour. The deadline
rows simply delete the variables that violate $T_i$ (fixing the bound to
$0$ is equivalent). The pairwise form $z_{i,p,q} + z_{i',p,q'} \le 1$ for
$i \ne i'$ and $\abs{q - q'} < 2$ is also correct but needs many more rows
and gives a weaker relaxation.

For $m = 6$ drones, two pads and $Q = 8$ slots there are $6 \cdot 2 \cdot 8
= 96$ binaries, $6$ assignment rows, $2 \cdot 8 = 16$ pad-slot rows and
$2 \cdot 7 = 14$ consecutive-slot rows, minus the deadline variables that
are fixed to zero (the pairwise form would need $2 \cdot 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5
= 420$ rows in place of the $14$). Two pads with the two-slot rule take at
most $2 \lceil Q/2 \rceil = 8$ landings, so six drones fit if their
deadlines allow. The relaxation of this program is tight -- the constraint
matrix is close to an assignment matrix -- which is why scheduling is the
one place in this chapter where a MILP of this size is solved in
milliseconds.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-mapfilp}
Agent~1 has one binary per edge of the time-expanded graph: from each
state $(v,t)$ with $t < 2$ there is a wait edge and one move edge per
neighbour on the cycle, so $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 24$ binaries per agent.
Flow conservation at $(a,0)$: $f^1_{(a,0)\to(a,1)} + f^1_{(a,0)\to(b,1)}
+ f^1_{(a,0)\to(c,1)} = 1$; at $(b,1)$: outgoing minus incoming equals
$0$; at $(d,2)$: incoming equals $1$. Capacity of $(b,1)$:
$f^1_{(a,0)\to(b,1)} + f^1_{(b,0)\to(b,1)} + f^1_{(d,0)\to(b,1)} +
(\text{the same for agent }2) \le 1$. Anti-swap on $\set{a,b}$ at $t = 0$:
$f^1_{(a,0)\to(b,1)} + f^1_{(b,0)\to(a,1)} + f^2_{(a,0)\to(b,1)} +
f^2_{(b,0)\to(a,1)} \le 1$. The drawn flows use $(a,0)\to(b,1)\to(d,2)$ and
$(d,0)\to(c,1)\to(a,2)$: every state is entered at most once and no edge is
used in both directions. The dashed alternative $(d,0)\to(b,1)$ makes the
capacity row of $(b,1)$ equal to $2$. On the $32 \times 32$ grid an interior state has five
outgoing edges (four moves and a wait), but a state on an edge of the grid
has four and a corner state three, so one layer of the time-expanded graph
has $\sum_v (\deg v + 1) = 1024 + 2 \cdot (2 \cdot 32 \cdot 31) = 4992$
edges. That gives $20 \cdot 60 \cdot 4992 \approx 6.0 \cdot 10^6$ binaries,
the ``about six million'' of \cref{sec:ch22-mapf-ilp}; $1024 \cdot 60
\approx 6.1 \cdot 10^4$ capacity rows; and $1984 \cdot 60 \approx 1.2 \cdot
10^5$ anti-swap rows, one per undirected grid edge and step, the grid
having $2 \cdot 32 \cdot 31 = 1984$ edges.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch22-coding}
\begin{sloppypar}
Hints. (a)~Build an \code{Instance} whose three \code{Vehicle} entries are
$(1,1) \to (9,7)$, $(1,4) \to (9,4)$ and $(1,7) \to (9,1)$, with the
obstacle $[4,6] \times [2,6]$, the workspace $[0,10] \times [0,8]$,
\code{horizon=12}, \code{dt=0.5}, \code{a\_max=2}, \code{v\_max=3},
\code{d\_min=1} and \code{objective="fuel"}; pass it to
\code{solve\_instance(inst, time\_limit=120)} and repeat the run with
\code{objective="time"}. The fuel model
has $3$ obstacle groups and $3$ pair groups per step, hence $4 \cdot 12
\cdot 6 = 288$ binaries among $588$ variables and $672$ rows. The reference
run finishes inside the limit with \code{sol.status} $= 0$: the fuel
optimum is $J_{\mathrm{fuel}} = 21.19$~m/s after $9\,755$ branch-and-bound
nodes and about $44$~s, tens of times the two-drone instance of
\cref{ex:ch22-two}. The minimum-time model ($324$ binaries) is much easier,
$11$ nodes and about $2$~s, with arrival times summing to $13.5$~s
(objective $13.535$ including the fuel tie-breaker). If your run hits the
limit, report \code{sol.status} $= 1$ and \code{sol.gap} instead of
concluding that the model is wrong. \code{continuous\_check} shows what the
coarse $\dt$ costs: the fuel plan cuts the block by $0.45$~m and its
separation drops to $0.83$~m between samples, so the margins of
\cref{prop:ch22-intersample} are needed before such a plan is flown. (b)~A separation constraint is active at step $k$ when
\code{check\_solution}-style distances give $\norm{\pos^i_k -
\pos^j_k}_\infty = d_{\min}$ within $10^{-6}$. (c)~With the limit the
status is $1$ and \code{sol.gap} holds the relative gap; the scaling
experiment of the chapter obtained gaps below $10\,\%$ after $30$~s for
this instance. (d)~Fix the bound of the binary
\code{model.cpair((0, 1), k, 0)} to $1$ for all $k$ before calling
\code{model.solve}. By the convention of \cref{eq:ch22-sep} this
\emph{switches off} the row ``drone~1 at least $d_{\min}$ to the right of
drone~2'', so that pair must be separated in one of the three other
directions at every step and the mirror-image subtree disappears; compare
\code{sol.nodes} and \code{sol.solve\_time}. The optimum is unchanged only
when an optimal plan that never relies on that direction exists, so check
the objective against the unconstrained run before trusting the faster
one.
\end{sloppypar}
\end{solution}
