\begin{solution}{exr:ch20-coding}
Checkpoints. The pipeline is in \code{ch20\_\allowbreak prediction.py}: \code{generate\_\allowbreak dataset} and
\code{prepare\_\allowbreak sequences} (the agent frame of \cref{eq:ch20-frame}), \code{predict\_\allowbreak cv},
\code{predict\_\allowbreak ca} and \code{predict\_\allowbreak kf}, \code{Seq2SeqPredictor} with
\code{train\_\allowbreak predictor}, and \code{evaluate} with its per-horizon variant;
\code{run\_\allowbreak experiment} produces the whole table in under a minute. The data are one array
of shape $(N,T_{\mathrm{obs}}+T_{\mathrm{pred}},2)$ plus a family label per trajectory,
split \emph{by trajectory} and never by time step. Steps: metrics first, implementing
\cref{eq:ch20-ade-fde} and checking ADE and FDE by hand on a two-step example; baselines
next, tuning their one knob (the smoothing window $k$, the process noise $q$) on
validation only; then the network, predicting displacements in the agent frame as a
residual over constant velocity; finally repeat every learned run over at least three
seeds and report a mean and a spread. Expected numbers at the $2.4$~s horizon with
$\sigma=0.05$~m: ADE $1.23$ for raw two-point CV, $1.14$ tuned, $1.13$ for the tuned
Kalman filter and $0.79$--$0.82$ for the LSTM; per horizon the tuned CV runs $0.30$,
$0.68$, $1.14$ at $0.8$, $1.6$ and $2.4$~s against $0.23$, $0.48$, $0.79$. Look hardest
at the \emph{straight} subset, where the tuned CV wins ($0.34$ against $0.41$): the
network buys its average at the turns and the evasive manoeuvres. Training is about
fifteen seconds per model in NumPy, so three seeds are cheap; in~(b) expect the gap to
shrink as $\sigma$ grows and to widen with the training set, with $400$ trajectories the
point where the LSTM starts to lose. Classic mistakes: leakage between splits; predicting
absolute coordinates; a straw-man baseline built from two raw points; comparing at
different horizons; tuning anything on the test set. Done when you have an ADE/FDE table
per horizon and method over several seeds and one honest sentence about it --- ``the LSTM
did not beat constant velocity at $0.8$~s'' is a legitimate result, and the Week~10
milestone (\cref{ch:appA}) asks for exactly that.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch20-calibration}
Checkpoints. Three functions do the bookkeeping: \code{calibration} returns the fraction
of true positions inside the $p$-ellipse per horizon step, \code{chi2\_\allowbreak radius} is the
Mahalanobis radius for a given $p$, and \code{inflated\_\allowbreak radius} turns a covariance into
the single obstacle radius of \cref{eq:ch20-inflated} that \orca can take. Keep
$\gamma_h$ as a vector of length $T_{\mathrm{pred}}$, one factor per horizon step, fitted
on validation and then frozen. Steps: measure the coverage you actually have before
fitting anything --- on the chapter's data the tuned Kalman filter contains $0.85$,
$0.81$ and $0.79$ of the true positions at $0.8$, $1.6$ and $2.4$~s and the LSTM-NLL head
only $0.76$, $0.63$ and $0.54$, so both are overconfident and the network far more so;
fit $\gamma_h$ by bisection on the empirical coverage, expecting roughly $1.25$ to $1.4$
for the Kalman filter and $1.45$ to $2.0$ for the LSTM, growing with $h$; verify on test,
where a factor fitted on validation should land within a couple of points of $95\,\%$,
and if it does not your split leaks; feed \code{inflated\_\allowbreak radius} into the \orca loop of
\cref{ch:ch13} as the intruder radius and compare three predictors on the same seeds on
two numbers only, the minimum separation and how many steps the avoidance layer was
active. The expected shape of the result is a trade: a bigger radius buys separation and
costs manoeuvres, so report both or the comparison is empty. For~(c),
\code{toy\_\allowbreak agent\_\allowbreak attention} and \code{multi\_\allowbreak head\_\allowbreak attention} are the NumPy pieces, and
two crossing drones are the smallest scene where a pooled or attended neighbour state can
help at all. Classic mistakes: one global $\gamma$ instead of one per step; fitting
$\gamma$ on test; inflating by the trace instead of the largest eigenvalue; comparing
avoidance counts across different intruder trajectories. Done when the calibrated
ellipses hold $95\,\%$ on held-out data and your \orca table shows what the extra radius
bought and what it cost.
\end{solution}
