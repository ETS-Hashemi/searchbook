% Hints for the coding exercise(s) of ch24 (assembled into Appendix C)

\begin{solution}{exr:ch24-coding}
Checkpoints for the capstone; \code{ch24\_\allowbreak hybrid.py} is the reference build, not the one
to copy. Its shape is the advice: one \code{HybridSimulation} owns the clock, the grid
and the drones; one \code{Drone} record holds the nominal path, the mode and the
reconnection bookkeeping; the four layers appear as four methods --- \code{cbs} before
take-off, \code{IntruderTracker} every cycle, \code{local\_\allowbreak layer} every cycle,
\code{replan} on demand --- behind exactly the interfaces of \cref{tab:ch24-layers},
with the executive in \code{decide}, \code{try\_\allowbreak reconnect} and \code{recheck} and the
rates kept apart by \code{RateSchedule}. Steps: fix the world model of
\cref{sec:ch24-world} first, with one conversion between grid cells at integer time and
continuous positions at real time, written once; get the state machine of
\cref{fig:ch24-state-machine} running with a perfect predictor before any noise; add the
reconnection test of \cref{def:ch24-reconnection} and the re-check, then the replanner;
swap the fresh space-time \astar for \dstarlite (\cref{ch:ch05}) and count expansions per
repair against a fresh search, which is the whole answer to~(b); make the three policies
three objects with one interface, so the runner of \cref{ch:ch25} cannot tell them apart.
Expected numbers on the chapter's scenario: the nominal plan costs $\sumcost=35$ with a
makespan of $13$ after two CT nodes; the intruder is first seen at $t=1.3$~s; drone~A
leaves \emph{Nominal} at $3.5$~s and drone~C at $4.3$~s; one reconnection succeeds with a
delay of one step and two fail on a blocked segment, each triggering a replan, so the run
ends with one replan for A, one for C and no collision; the minimum separation is
$1.20$~m to the intruder and $0.80$~m between teammates; the formation error peaks at
$2.10$~m and ends at $0.18$~m; the radio graph never disconnects; \orca is infeasible on
two cycles; the $20$~s run is about $200$ cycles and a second of \python. Classic
mistakes: one file that mixes the layers; two different time steps for the grid plan and
the continuous loop; a reconnection accepted without re-checking teammates; a re-check
run before the replan instead of after. Done when the matrix of~(e) runs unattended from
one script, every run is reproducible from its seed, and your one-page conclusion says
which policy wins where --- the Week~12 milestone of \cref{ch:appA}.
\end{solution}
