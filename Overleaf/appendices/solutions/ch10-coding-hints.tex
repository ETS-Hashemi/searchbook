\begin{solution}{exr:ch10-coding}
Checkpoints. Two functions of \code{ch10\_\allowbreak ecbs.py} are the algorithm:
\code{focal\_\allowbreak space\_\allowbreak time\_\allowbreak astar} (\cref{alg:ch10-lowlevel}) and \code{ecbs}
(\cref{alg:ch10-ecbs}); \code{OtherPaths} answers ``how many other agents does this state
hit'' in constant time, and \code{cbs} in the same file is the $w=1$ reference. Each level
needs \emph{two} orderings of the same nodes: a heap on $\fcost$ (or on $\sumcost$) that
owns the lower bound of \cref{eq:ch10-LB}, and a heap on the conflict count $h_c$
restricted to the band $\fcost\le w\fcost_{\min}$ of \cref{eq:ch10-band}. Steps: make the
low level return the pair (path, $\fcost_{\min}$), because the high-level bound is the sum
of those minima and never the sum of the returned path costs; add $h_c$ and the focal
choice downstairs; add the same band upstairs; wrap the benchmark in a runner that writes
one row per (instance, solver) and validates before it records. Expected numbers on the
$8\times8$ grids of \cref{fig:ch10-benchmark}: at $k=10$ \cbs needs $221$ CT nodes and
$0.22$~s against $2.2$ nodes and $3$~ms for $\ecbs(1.5)$; at $k=16$ \cbs solves a fifth of
the instances in about $3$~s while $\ecbs(1.5)$ solves all of them in $23$~ms at a mean
$\cost/C^*$ of $1.07$. The knee sits near $w\approx1.1$--$1.2$, and the largest ratio you
are likely to see is about $1.3$ for $w=1.5$: the guarantee of \cref{thm:ch10-ecbs} is
sound but loose, and saying so with your own numbers is the point of~(c). Classic
mistakes: using the popped node's $\fcost$ as the band reference instead of the minimum
over \Open; taking the returned path cost as the lower bound; never re-growing the focal
list when $\fcost_{\min}$ rises; averaging $\cost/C^*$ over instances \cbs failed on. Done
when $w=1$ reproduces the optimal costs exactly and one paragraph says, with numbers, what
your speed-up cost --- the Week~5 milestone (\cref{ch:appA}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch10-variants}
Checkpoints. All three parts are small edits to \code{ch10\_\allowbreak ecbs.py}, so copy it once and
keep the four variants side by side in one benchmark script. (a)~$\mathrm{BCBS}(w,1)$ is
the band upstairs only: the low level goes back to plain \astar on $\fcost$ and returns
the cheapest path, ignoring $h_c$; $\mathrm{BCBS}(1,w)$ is the mirror image. Run all four
at $w=1.5$ on the benchmark of \cref{exr:ch10-coding} and plot CT expansions and
$\cost/C^*$ together; expect the low-level band to do most of the work, because a path of
equal cost with fewer conflicts removes constraint-tree nodes before they are ever
generated, while the high-level band mostly reorders nodes that already exist. (b)~The
duplicate rule is one line (line~\ref{alg:ch10-lowlevel:dup}): a state already expanded
with an equal $\gcost$ is dropped today, and you want it re-inserted when the new path has
a smaller $h_c$, so keep the closed entry as a pair $(\gcost,h_c)$ and dominate on both.
On \cref{ex:ch10-grid} the root has cost $11$, per-agent bounds $(2,4,5)$ and one
conflict, and $\ecbs(1.2)$ returns $13$ after one split; the question is whether the new
rule finds an equal-cost conflict-free path for one of the three agents, which would make
the root itself the answer at cost $11$. (c)~Expect a small gain that grows with $k$, and
report it as a paired comparison on the same seeds. Classic mistakes: admitting a state
with a \emph{larger} $\gcost$, which breaks \cref{thm:ch10-lowlevel-lb}; treating $h_c$ as
static when it is measured against the other agents' current paths; comparing variants at
different time limits. Done when the four variants are one flag apart and your table says
which level the band pays off at.
\end{solution}
