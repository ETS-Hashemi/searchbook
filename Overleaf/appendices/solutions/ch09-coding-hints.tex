\begin{solution}{exr:ch09-coding}
Checkpoints. The reference is \code{ch09\_\allowbreak cbs.py}: \code{low\_\allowbreak level}
(\cref{alg:ch09-lowlevel}), \code{cbs} (\cref{alg:ch09-cbs}), \code{all\_\allowbreak conflicts},
\code{validate}, and \code{print\_\allowbreak trace} to compare your log against
\cref{tab:ch09-ct}. A constraint-tree node holds one immutable constraint set per agent,
one path per agent and their sum of costs; the high level is a heap keyed on that sum
with the conflict count as tie-break, and a child copies its parent's set plus one entry.
Steps: write the low level alone --- state $(v,t)$, wait action, vertex and edge
constraints, the goal-stay test of \cref{eq:ch09-tgamma}, a horizon --- and check it
against your unconstrained space-time \astar of \cref{ch:ch04}; reuse the Week-3 checker
for detection with parked agents; add the high-level loop; certify tiny instances against
a brute-force search of the joint space. Expected numbers: on \cref{ex:ch09-crossing} the
root costs $10$, the tree generates seven nodes, expands four, calls the low level nine
times and returns $12$. On random $8\times8$ grids with $15\,\%$ obstacles
(\cref{fig:ch09-scaling}) expect about $1.3$ CT nodes and $0.4$~ms at $k=2$, $4$ nodes
and $1.4$~ms at $k=4$, and $55$ nodes, $1\,300$ low-level expansions and $0.45$~s at
$k=8$, where one instance in twelve already misses a five-second limit; by $k=12$ the
mean is $600$ nodes. Classic mistakes: an edge constraint stored without its direction or
time; a low level that returns at the first pop of the goal; sorting the high level by
makespan while reporting $\sumcost$; missing conflicts after the shorter path has ended.
Done when your plans pass the Week-3 checker, your costs equal the reference everywhere,
and a hand-drawn two-agent tree matches your log --- the Week~4 milestone
(\cref{ch:appA}).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exr:ch09-icbs-coding}
Checkpoints. Build on \cref{exr:ch09-coding}; the reference is the cardinal branch of
\code{\_\allowbreak choose\_\allowbreak conflict} and \code{\_\allowbreak bypass} in \code{ch09\_\allowbreak cbs.py}. The cheap
classifier: scan a node's conflicts in time order, generate \emph{both} children of each,
and rank the conflict by how many children cost more than the parent --- two means
cardinal (\cref{def:ch09-cardinal}), one semi-cardinal, zero non-cardinal; stop at the
first cardinal one and keep the children you built, or you pay for them twice. The
bypass: if a child has the parent's cost and fewer conflicts, adopt its paths in the
parent and do not split. The MDD classifier: two breadth-first searches in space-time per
agent, forward from the start and backward from the goal, keep the states on some optimal
path, and read cardinality off the two widths at the conflict's time step, with no child
generation at all. Expected numbers on the $8\times8$ benchmark of
\cref{fig:ch09-scaling}: at $k=8$ the mean falls from about $55$ CT nodes and $0.45$~s to
$15$ nodes and $0.045$~s, with success rising to $1.0$; at $k=12$, from $622$ to $92$
nodes. Report the ratio: low-level expansions per CT node rise from roughly $23$ to
$170$, which is what the node count was bought with, and why the MDD variant is worth the
code. On \cref{ex:ch09-crossing} nothing changes --- four expansions, seven nodes, no
bypass --- so do not tune on it. Classic mistakes: counting probe children as expansions;
a bypass that changes a node's cost; MDDs built to the unconstrained depth instead of the
agent's current optimal cost. Done when both classifiers agree on every conflict of a few
hundred random nodes and your scaling plot shows the book's crossover.
\end{solution}
